Every carbon credit lives in a registry: an electronic ledger, run by or for a standard such as Verra or Gold Standard, that records who holds each unit and whether it has been used. When you buy, the units have to end up somewhere on that ledger. You have two choices. Either they move into a registry account in your own name, or the seller retires them on your behalf and names you as the beneficiary.

On our desk both routes are available. At the transfer stage, units either go into the buyer's own registry account or are retired on the buyer's behalf in the name of the beneficiary they choose. This page helps you decide which suits you.

## What "retirement on behalf" actually means

When credits are retired, they are permanently taken out of circulation so nobody can sell or use them again. The registry records the retirement against the serial numbers, along with a beneficiary name and often a short purpose statement.

The account holder who performs the retirement does not have to be the company making the claim. A seller can retire units from its own account and record your organisation as the beneficiary. The public registry entry then shows your name, and you receive a certificate. You never hold the units yourself, but the retirement is still made for you and can be checked by anyone.

## Option 1: Retirement on your behalf

**Who it suits:** companies buying for a one-off or annual voluntary goal, smaller organisations, and first-time buyers who want the credits used rather than held.

**Advantages**

- No account to open, fund or maintain.
- No need to learn the registry interface or manage user access.
- Fewer steps between purchase and proof, because the units do not move twice.

**Drawbacks**

- You depend on the account holder to carry out the retirement correctly, with the right beneficiary name and quantity. This is why the serial numbers and certificate matter so much; see [retirement certificates explained](/knowledge-base/retirement-certificates-explained/).
- You cannot hold the units and decide later. Once retired, they are used.
- You cannot resell, which matters if your plans change.

## Option 2: Your own registry account

**Who it suits:** airlines and aircraft operators meeting CORSIA obligations, large corporates buying across several years or projects, traders, and any buyer who wants to control timing.

**Advantages**

- The units sit under your control. You choose when to retire and what the retirement record says.
- You can buy now and retire later, for example buying in one financial year and retiring against the next year's inventory.
- You can hold units from several sellers in one place and see your whole position.
- For CORSIA, some operators prefer to cancel units themselves so the cancellation trail runs straight from their own account to their report to the national authority (the DGCA in India).

**Drawbacks**

- Opening an account takes time and paperwork.
- Some registries charge account or transaction fees; check the current schedule on the registry's own website.
- Someone in your organisation must look after access, approvals and security.
- Registries are separate, so if you buy Verra and Gold Standard units you may need an account on each.

## What opening an account generally involves

The details differ by registry and change over time, so always follow the registry's own current instructions. In general, expect:

1. **An application** naming the organisation, its registered address and the people who will use the account.
2. **Know-your-customer checks.** Registries usually ask for company incorporation documents, proof of address, details of directors or beneficial owners, and identity documents for the authorised users.
3. **Review and approval** by the registry administrator, which can involve follow-up questions.
4. **Fees and terms.** You accept the registry's terms of use and pay any opening or annual charges that apply.
5. **Set-up of users and permissions.** Many registries require more than one authorised person so that transfers and retirements need a second approval.

For an Indian company, the incorporation certificate, PAN, board resolution authorising the signatories and director identity documents are the sort of papers you might be asked for. Gather them early; account approval is often the slowest part of a first purchase.

For official guidance, start at [verra.org](https://verra.org/), [goldstandard.org](https://www.goldstandard.org/) or [globalcarboncouncil.com](https://www.globalcarboncouncil.com/), depending on which programme your units come from. ## A quick way to decide

| Your situation | Likely better route |
|---|---|
| First purchase, single voluntary goal | Retire on your behalf |
| Annual offsetting of a stable footprint | Retire on your behalf, or own account if volumes grow |
| Buying ahead and retiring in later years | Own account |
| CORSIA compliance | Own account is common; discuss with your authority |
| Want the option to resell | Own account |

Whichever route you take, the proof works the same way: serial numbers and a public registry record.
