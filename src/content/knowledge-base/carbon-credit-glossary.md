This glossary covers the words that come up on our listings, in seller paperwork and in airline compliance work. Each entry is kept short, with a link where we have a fuller guide. Terms are grouped by theme so related ideas sit together.

## The basics

**Carbon credit.** A tradable unit representing one tonne of carbon dioxide equivalent that has been kept out of, or taken out of, the atmosphere by a specific project.

**tCO₂e (tonne of carbon dioxide equivalent).** The common measure for greenhouse gases. Other gases, such as methane, are converted to the amount of CO₂ that would cause the same warming.

**Voluntary carbon market.** Buying and retiring credits by choice, for example to support a company's climate goal, rather than to meet a legal duty.

**Compliance market.** A market where buying units is required by law or regulation. CORSIA and national schemes fall here.

**Offsetting.** Using retired credits to balance emissions a buyer has not yet been able to cut.

## Project types

**Removal.** A project that takes CO₂ out of the air and stores it, such as new forest or mangrove planting. See [forestry and agroforestry credits](/knowledge-base/forestry-and-agroforestry-credits/).

**Reduction.** A project that lowers emissions compared with what would otherwise happen, such as a factory capturing waste heat.

**Avoidance.** A project that prevents emissions that were expected to occur, such as protecting a forest under threat or replacing coal-fired power with solar.

**Blue carbon.** Carbon held in coastal and marine ecosystems, chiefly mangroves, seagrass and salt marsh. See [blue carbon credits](/knowledge-base/blue-carbon-credits/).

**Biogas.** Gas from the breakdown of organic waste, which can be captured and burned for energy instead of escaping as methane. See [waste and biogas credits](/knowledge-base/waste-and-biogas-credits/).

**Improved cookstove.** A more efficient household stove that burns less wood or charcoal. See [agriculture and cookstove credits](/knowledge-base/agriculture-and-cookstove-credits/).

**fNRB (fraction of non-renewable biomass).** The share of wood use that is not being replaced by regrowth. It drives how many credits a cookstove project earns.

**Grid emission factor.** The average CO₂ released per unit of electricity on a given power grid, used to work out savings from renewable projects. See [renewable energy credits](/knowledge-base/renewable-energy-credits/).

## Quality tests

**Additionality.** The test of whether the project would have happened anyway. Credits only make sense if the carbon revenue made the difference. See [additionality, permanence and leakage](/knowledge-base/additionality-permanence-and-leakage/).

**Baseline.** The emissions that would have occurred without the project. Credits are measured against it.

**Permanence.** How long the stored or avoided carbon stays out of the atmosphere. A concern mainly for forests and soils, which can burn or be cleared.

**Leakage.** Emissions pushed elsewhere by the project, such as logging moving to the next valley when one forest is protected.

**Buffer pool.** A reserve of credits set aside from many projects to cover losses if stored carbon is later released.

**Co-benefits.** Gains beyond carbon, such as cleaner air, jobs or water supply. See [SDGs and co-benefits](/knowledge-base/sdgs-and-co-benefits/).

**SDGs (Sustainable Development Goals).** The seventeen UN goals. Listings show which ones a project supports.

**Double counting.** The same tonne being claimed by more than one party or target.

## Standards and documents

**Standard (crediting programme).** The organisation that sets rules for projects and issues credits. Examples include [Verra](https://verra.org/), [Gold Standard](https://www.goldstandard.org/) and [Global Carbon Council](https://www.globalcarboncouncil.com/). See [carbon standards compared](/knowledge-base/carbon-standards-compared/).

**VCS (Verified Carbon Standard).** Verra's main programme. Its credits are called VCUs, verified carbon units.

**Methodology.** The approved rulebook for a project type, setting out how to set the baseline, monitor results and count reductions.

**PDD (Project Design Document).** The founding description of a project: location, technology, methodology, baseline and monitoring plan.

**Validation.** An independent check, before a project starts earning credits, that its design meets the standard.

**Verification.** A periodic independent check that the claimed reductions actually happened, which leads to issuance.

**MRV (monitoring, reporting and verification).** The cycle of measuring results, writing them up and having them checked. Used for both projects and airlines.

See [project documents buyers receive](/knowledge-base/project-documents-buyers-receive/) for what each paper should show.

## Registries and transactions

**Registry.** The official database where credits are issued, held, moved and retired. It, not any seller, is the record of ownership. See [registries and serial numbers](/knowledge-base/carbon-registries-and-serial-numbers/).

**Registry ID.** The number a registry gives a project. Use it to look the project up yourself.

**Serial number.** The unique code for each credit or block of credits, so no tonne can be used twice.

**Issuance.** The moment a registry creates credits after a successful verification.

**Retirement.** Permanently taking a credit out of circulation so its benefit can be claimed. See [retirement certificates explained](/knowledge-base/retirement-certificates-explained/).

**Cancellation.** In CORSIA, removing units against an airline's obligation. Often used interchangeably with retirement.

**Beneficiary.** The organisation named on a retirement as the one claiming the credit.

**Retirement certificate.** The registry document confirming a retirement, its serial numbers and beneficiary.

## Buying and selling

**Vintage.** The year the reduction or removal took place. See [choosing vintage and volume](/knowledge-base/choosing-vintage-and-volume/).

**Lot.** A defined quantity of credits from one project and vintage, offered at one price.

**Spot.** Credits already issued and ready to deliver now.

**Issued.** Credits that exist on the registry; delivery timing is agreed case by case.

**Forward.** Credits expected from future verification, sold before they exist. See [spot, issued and forward credits](/knowledge-base/spot-issued-and-forward-credits/).

**Offtake agreement.** A contract to buy a project's future credits over a period. See [forward sales and offtake agreements](/knowledge-base/forward-sales-and-offtake-agreements/).

**Indicative price.** A per-tonne price shown for guidance, firm only once confirmed in writing. See [how carbon credit prices are quoted](/knowledge-base/how-carbon-credit-prices-are-quoted/).

**Due diligence.** The checks a buyer runs on a project, its documents and the seller before paying. See [what buyers check before buying](/knowledge-base/what-buyers-check-before-buying/).

**Aggregator.** A business that bundles many small projects or sellers into larger lots.

## CORSIA and international rules

**CORSIA.** ICAO's carbon offsetting and reduction scheme for international aviation, adopted in 2016. See [CORSIA explained for airlines](/knowledge-base/corsia-explained-for-airlines/).

**ICAO.** The UN agency for civil aviation, which runs CORSIA. Official material is on [icao.int](https://www.icao.int/).

**DGCA.** India's Directorate General of Civil Aviation, the national authority for Indian operators under CORSIA.

**MTOM (maximum take-off mass).** The certified top weight for take-off. Only aeroplanes above 5,700 kg are counted.

**Emissions monitoring plan.** An airline's approved description of how it will measure fuel and CO₂.

**Offsetting requirement.** The volume of units an airline must cancel for a compliance period. Try our [calculator](/calculator/).

**Growth factor.** The figure ICAO publishes to translate an airline's emissions into its share of sector growth.

**Compliance period.** The block of years over which offsetting is totalled and settled. See the [compliance calendar](/knowledge-base/corsia-compliance-calendar/).

**CORSIA-eligible unit.** A unit from an approved programme, in the right vintage window, with any required authorisation. See the [eligible units checklist](/knowledge-base/corsia-eligible-units-checklist/).

**CORSIA eligible fuel.** Sustainable or lower-carbon aviation fuel whose savings can be deducted from the offsetting requirement.

**Article 6.** The part of the Paris Agreement that lets countries cooperate, including by trading emission reductions.

**Host-country authorisation.** A government's formal approval for credits from its territory to be used abroad or in schemes like CORSIA.

**Corresponding adjustment.** The accounting entry a host country makes so an exported tonne no longer counts towards its own target. See [corresponding adjustments made simple](/knowledge-base/corresponding-adjustments-made-simple/).

**CCTS (Carbon Credit Trading Scheme).** India's national carbon market framework, which covers certain industrial sectors.
