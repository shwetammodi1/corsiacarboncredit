These are the questions that come across our desk most weeks, from airline compliance teams, corporate buyers and Indian project developers. Where a question has a one-word answer, it is in the table below. The longer ones are grouped by who usually asks them.

![Illustration used across the CORSIA knowledge base](/images/corsia/hero-corsia-knowledge-base.svg)

## Quick answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Are domestic flights covered? | No. International flights only. National policy may cover domestic flying, CORSIA does not. |
| Are helicopters covered? | No. The scheme applies to aeroplanes. |
| Can EU ETS allowances be used for CORSIA? | No. They belong to a different system. |
| Can surplus cancellations roll into the next period? | No. Extra cancelled units are simply spent. |
| Can our consultant also verify our report? | No. The verifier must be independent of whoever prepared the work. |
| Does holding eligible units satisfy the obligation? | No. Units must be cancelled, and the cancellation reported. |
| Does ICAO certify CORSIA consultants? | No. That credential does not exist. |
| Does programme approval make every unit from it eligible? | No. Vintage and corresponding adjustment are tested unit by unit. |

## If you operate aircraft

::: accordion Is taking part in CORSIA optional?
Two different things get called "participation". A **State** could choose whether to join during the pilot and first phase; from 2027 most States are required to. An **operator** has no such choice once its State is in.

On the operator side, monitoring and reporting apply from the start to anyone above the threshold. Offsetting applies to flights between participating States.
:::

::: accordion How small can an operator be and still be in scope?
The line is more than 10,000 tonnes of CO2 a year from international flights, counting aeroplanes above 5,700 kg maximum certificated take-off mass. That is a modest operation. Work the number out from your own fuel data rather than guessing. [Scope and thresholds](/knowledge-base/corsia-scope-and-thresholds/) goes through it.
:::

::: accordion All our routes go to non-participating States. Is there anything to do?
You owe no offsets on those routes. You still monitor, report and verify, because every operator's data goes into the sectoral growth factor. Reporting is wide; offsetting is narrow.
:::

::: accordion Our group has several airlines. Which one is "the operator"?
Usually the entity whose Air Operator Certificate the flight operates under. A wet lease follows the operating certificate, not the owner of the aircraft. A code share sits with the carrier that flies it, not the one that sells the seat. Several certificates in one group means several operators, each assessed on its own numbers.
:::

::: accordion Who at our end is legally on the hook?
The operator. A consultant prepares, a verifier checks, an intermediary sources units. None of them takes over the accountability. Someone in-house has to be able to explain your data without calling an outside firm.
:::

::: accordion What are the penalties if we do not comply?
National law sets them, not ICAO, so they differ by State. They have included fines, public naming of non-compliant operators and, in serious cases, conditions attached to operating permissions. For an Indian carrier the DGCA is the body to ask.
:::

## Cost and the calculation

::: accordion What will CORSIA cost us?
That depends on four things: your covered emissions, the growth factor, any fuels claim and the price of units. None of them can be answered in general. Three practical rules: budget from covered emissions, not total emissions; work with a range, not a single number; and model the 2027 change against your actual routes. The arithmetic is in [how the offsetting requirement is calculated](/knowledge-base/corsia-offsetting-requirement-calculation/).
:::

::: accordion Our emissions went down. Why do we still owe anything?
Through 2029, the obligation uses the **sectoral** growth factor. If the sector as a whole grew, you apply that growth percentage to your own covered emissions, even if your own figure fell.
:::

::: accordion What happens in 2027 and in 2030?
In 2027 participation becomes compulsory for States above set aviation activity thresholds, which widens covered route pairs considerably. How much that matters to you depends entirely on your network, so model your routes rather than adding a flat percentage.

From 2030 the individual growth factor starts to count, at 15% weight, rising to 30% from 2033. Carriers growing faster than the sector will carry more of the burden.
:::

::: accordion Can you give us a price per tonne?
Not honestly without knowing project type, vintage, volume, contract structure and authorisation status. A quote that ignores those is not for anything you could actually buy. Voluntary market averages are a poor guide, and not by a small margin.
:::

## If you are buying units

::: accordion We already buy carbon credits for our ESG programme. Does that count?
Very likely not. Most voluntary credits fall short of the CORSIA criteria, above all because the host State has not made a corresponding adjustment. Buying eligible units is a separate exercise, in a separate market, at a separate price.
:::

::: accordion What does a unit need in order to qualify?
Two things. It must be issued under an ICAO-approved crediting programme, **and** it must meet the Emissions Unit Criteria in its own right: additionality, conservative quantification, permanence, verification, no double counting (through a corresponding adjustment), an eligible vintage, no net harm, and traceability in a registry.
:::

::: accordion A seller describes units as "CORSIA-ready". Is that good enough?
Read it as "not eligible". "CORSIA-ready", "CORSIA-aligned" and "eligible pending authorisation" are marketing phrases, not standards, and all describe units without host-State authorisation. Buying them means accepting sovereign risk. That can be a deliberate choice; it should never be an accidental one.
:::

::: accordion Why is there so little eligible supply?
The corresponding adjustment. The host government has to add the tonnes back into its national accounts and give them up against its own climate target. Many decline, and many have no procedure. That alone rules out most of the world's credits. [Corresponding adjustments and Article 6](/knowledge-base/corresponding-adjustments-article-6/) has the detail.
:::

::: accordion Can a voluntary credit be turned into a CORSIA credit?
Not by anything the holder does. If the programme is approved, the vintage is eligible and the host State authorises those particular units with a corresponding adjustment, they can become eligible. That is a government decision. Nobody can sell it to you as a service.
:::

::: accordion When should we buy, and what if we cannot find supply?
Model your obligation every year and buy in stages. At the end of each period every operator reaches the same decision in the same few months, in a constrained market, so waiting is a bet the whole sector places together.

If you cannot find eligible supply, raise it early with your authority and your adviser, and make sure supply contracts say what happens if units cannot be delivered.
:::

### How long a first purchase takes

Allow three to six months end to end, assuming nothing goes wrong:

| Step | Typical time |
|---|---|
| Registry account opening | 4 to 8 weeks |
| Finding supply | 2 to 6 weeks |
| Due diligence | 1 to 3 weeks per tranche |
| Contracting | 2 to 6 weeks |
| Transfer | 1 to 3 weeks |
| Cancellation and reporting | 1 to 2 weeks |

## If you develop or sell credits

::: accordion Can any carbon project sell to airlines under CORSIA?
No. The methodology must sit under an ICAO-approved programme, and the host State must authorise the units and apply a corresponding adjustment. That second requirement removes many projects that are otherwise very good.
:::

::: accordion Can an Indian project supply CORSIA?
Only if India authorises the transfer with a corresponding adjustment. India is building its domestic Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS), and how that fits with Article 6 authorisation is still being settled. Confirm the route to authorisation before you commit to a methodology. See [CORSIA in India](/knowledge-base/corsia-in-india/).
:::

::: accordion How long before a new project can supply?
Expect eighteen months to three years from concept to first issuance. Host-State authorisation is the step whose timing is hardest to predict.
:::

::: accordion What will an airline buyer ask us for?
In a single reply: the host-State authorisation document, evidence of programme approval, vintage confirmation, the verification report and registry records. Site photographs and community stories help sell voluntary credits. They do not answer a compliance buyer's question.
:::

## Fuels and other schemes

::: accordion Does flying on SAF take us out of CORSIA?
No. It lowers the obligation, and only if the fuel meets the CORSIA Eligible Fuels criteria, is certified under an approved scheme and has a documented chain of custody. Qualifying fuel without the paperwork helps the climate and earns no CORSIA claim.
:::

::: accordion Is SAF cheaper than buying units?
Usually not yet. Per tonne of CO2 avoided, eligible fuel generally costs more than eligible units today, because of the fuel's price premium over conventional jet fuel. The gap depends on that premium, on unit prices and on how much documentation the claim needs, so run the comparison on current numbers rather than on an old estimate.
:::

::: accordion Can one batch of SAF be claimed under both CORSIA and the EU ETS?
No. Double claiming across schemes is precisely what verifiers look for. Settle which scheme a batch will be claimed under before you sign the fuel contract.
:::

::: accordion Has CORSIA replaced the EU ETS?
No. One operator can be subject to both. They use different instruments, credits in one and allowances in the other, and their scopes are set to limit double obligation on the same emissions. Map your network against both.
:::

::: accordion Can units cancelled for CORSIA also back a carbon-neutral claim?
No. They have already discharged a legal obligation. Using them again for marketing is a double claim, and disclosure regulators are paying closer attention to exactly this.
:::

## Where to start

If you are new to the scheme, do things in this order: confirm scope and your threshold position, then get the monitoring plan right, and only then think about credits. Mistakes in the plan repeat every year and tend to come out at verification, when that year can no longer be redone. If you are starting from zero, read [what CORSIA is](/knowledge-base/what-is-corsia/) first.

When choosing advisers, remember that ICAO does not certify consultants. What does exist is accreditation of **verification bodies** and ICAO approval of **crediting programmes**. Anyone calling themselves "ICAO certified" or "CORSIA certified" is claiming something that does not exist, which is itself useful information.
