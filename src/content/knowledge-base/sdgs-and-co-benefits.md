Each listing on our marketplace shows a row of UN Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) tiles. A cookstove project might show good health, gender equality and life on land. A mangrove project might show life below water and no poverty. These tiles describe the project's co-benefits: the good it does beyond cutting or removing carbon. This article explains where they come from, how to tell a claimed benefit from a proven one, and how to fit them into a buying decision.

## The seventeen goals in one paragraph

The United Nations adopted seventeen SDGs in 2015, covering poverty, hunger, health, education, gender, water, energy, work, industry, inequality, cities, consumption, climate, oceans, land, peace and partnerships. Every carbon project contributes to SDG 13, climate action, by definition. The other tiles are where projects start to look different from one another.

## What co-benefits look like in practice

| Category | Typical co-benefits | Usual SDGs |
|---|---|---|
| Agriculture and cookstoves | Less smoke indoors, less time collecting fuel, savings for households | 3, 5, 7, 15 |
| Forestry and agroforestry | Income from fruit or timber, soil and water protection, habitat | 1, 8, 15 |
| Blue carbon | Fisheries nurseries, storm protection for coastal villages | 1, 14 |
| Waste and biogas | Cleaner water, less odour, organic fertiliser for farms | 3, 6, 11 |
| Renewable energy | Local construction jobs, more reliable power | 7, 8 |
| Industrial efficiency | Better air quality near plants, lower energy bills | 9, 12 |

These are typical patterns, not promises. The tiles on a specific listing should reflect that project's own documents.

## Claimed, described or verified

Not every SDG tile carries the same evidence. We find it useful to sort them into three levels.

1. **Claimed.** The developer says the project supports a goal, usually in the PDD's description. No one has measured it. Most tiles start here.
2. **Monitored.** The project tracks an indicator for that goal, such as the number of households using stoves, hectares planted with native species, or local people employed, and reports it at each verification.
3. **Certified.** An auditor checks the indicator against a defined framework and the result is labelled on the registry. Gold Standard builds SDG impact into its core rules and can issue certified SDG outcomes. Verra offers optional labels, such as the Climate, Community & Biodiversity standards and SD VISta, for projects that want this level of assurance.

A tile backed by a monitored, audited indicator is worth far more than a tile that appears only in the project description.

## How to check a listing's SDGs

- **Find the indicator.** Ask which measurable indicator sits behind each tile. If there is none, treat the tile as aspirational.
- **Look in the monitoring report.** Verification and monitoring reports should show the indicator values for the period, with the method used.
- **Check for a label.** On the public registry, see whether the project carries a co-benefit label or certified SDG outcomes, and for which period.
- **Compare against the activity.** A solar farm claiming "zero hunger" or a landfill gas project claiming "quality education" needs a clear explanation. Unusual tiles are not wrong, but they should be specific.
- **Look for safeguards, not just benefits.** A good project also shows it did no harm: a working grievance process, free and informed consultation with communities, and fair benefit-sharing where land is involved.

## How much weight to give them

Co-benefits do not change the tonne. A credit with five strong SDG tiles is still one tonne of CO₂ equivalent, and it must first pass the carbon quality tests described in [additionality, permanence and leakage](/knowledge-base/additionality-permanence-and-leakage/). Strong co-benefits cannot rescue a weak carbon claim.

Once the carbon side is sound, co-benefits are a legitimate reason to choose one lot over another, and to pay more for it. They matter most when:

- your organisation's reporting covers social as well as environmental impact
- you want a story your employees and customers can understand, such as clean cooking for rural households in Odisha
- you operate in or source from the region where the project sits

For CORSIA, co-benefits do not affect eligibility. An airline still needs the right programme, vintage and authorisation. Some airlines choose projects with strong co-benefits anyway, for their own sustainability reporting.

## A note on communication

If you plan to talk publicly about co-benefits, keep to what was measured. Say "the project reports that 4,000 households now cook with improved stoves" only if the monitoring report says so, and keep the report on file.
