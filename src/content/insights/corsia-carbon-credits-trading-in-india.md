**How does CORSIA carbon credits trading work in India?** CORSIA is ICAO's offsetting scheme for international aviation. Under it, airlines must **cancel eligible emissions units**. Indian project developers can supply these units, but **only** if the credits come from an **ICAO-approved programme** and meet **unit-level conditions**. For the first phase, those conditions include **host-country authorisation** from India with a **corresponding adjustment**. There is **no central CORSIA exchange**: units are traded through brokers, exchanges and direct deals, then transferred and cancelled in programme registries. India's own **Carbon Credit Certificates are a separate, domestic instrument**. Indian airlines will face CORSIA offsetting requirements from **2027**. For more background, see our [CORSIA Carbon Credits](https://corsiacarboncredit.in/) resources.

This guide explains the rules, the trading process for buyers and sellers, how India's domestic market fits in, and the real opportunities and challenges.

## Introduction to CORSIA Carbon Credit Trading in India

**CORSIA**, the **Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation**, was adopted by the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) in 2016. It aims to stabilise net CO₂ emissions from international aviation by requiring airlines to offset emissions growth.

Carbon credit trading matters to aviation because airlines cannot yet cut emissions fast enough with fuel efficiency and sustainable aviation fuel alone. **Eligible emissions units** let them compensate for the rest.

India connects with this market in three ways:

- **As a supplier:** India has a large base of carbon projects, many registered with international programmes.
- **As a host country:** India decides whether to **authorise** credits for international use and apply corresponding adjustments.
- **As an aviation market:** Indian airlines will face offsetting requirements from 2027.

Interest is growing because CORSIA units have been priced above the average voluntary credit, and buyers increasingly want **high-integrity, authorised** credits.

## What Is CORSIA and How Does It Work?

### ICAO's role

ICAO, a UN agency, developed CORSIA and sets its rules through the **Standards and Recommended Practices (Annex 16, Volume IV)**. The **ICAO Council**, advised by its **Technical Advisory Body (TAB)**, decides which emissions unit programmes are eligible. National authorities administer CORSIA for their airlines. In India, that is the **Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA)**.

### Why CORSIA was introduced

International aviation emissions fall outside national climate targets under the Paris Agreement. CORSIA provides a global approach, rather than a patchwork of national rules.

### Monitoring and reporting

Airline operators with international emissions above **10,000 tonnes of CO₂ a year** must **monitor, report and verify (MRV)** their emissions, mostly from fuel use. This has applied since 2019, **regardless of whether their State participates in offsetting**.

### Offsetting obligations

Offsetting is **separate from MRV**. It applies only to emissions on **routes between participating States**, above a baseline of **85% of 2019 emissions**. The obligation is calculated using sector-wide growth factors, and later increasingly airline-specific ones.

### Purchasing and cancelling units

Airlines buy eligible emissions units and **cancel** them in the relevant registry. They then report the cancellations to their national authority by the deadline for each compliance period.

### Implementation phases

- **Pilot phase (2021–2023):** voluntary participation
- **First phase (2024–2026):** voluntary participation
- **Second phase (2027–2035):** mandatory for most States, with exemptions for least developed countries, small island developing states, landlocked developing countries and States with very small shares of international aviation, unless they volunteer

**CORSIA does not apply automatically to every airline or every flight.** It depends on the route, the States involved and the operator's emissions.

## Understanding the Indian Carbon Credit Market

India is building a **domestic carbon market** under the **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS)**, notified in June 2023:

- **Indian Carbon Market (ICM):** the national framework for the CCTS.
- **Ministry of Power:** the lead ministry.
- **Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE):** the scheme administrator.
- **Central Electricity Regulatory Commission (CERC):** regulates trading. Its trading regulations were notified in February 2026.
- **Grid Controller of India:** the registry.

The CCTS has two parts:

- **Compliance mechanism:** obligated industries get emissions-intensity targets and earn **Carbon Credit Certificates (CCCs)** if they beat them.
- **Offset mechanism:** non-obligated entities can register eligible projects, using BEE-approved methodologies, to earn CCCs.

CCCs will trade on power exchanges. **As of November 2026, large-scale trading had not started**, and a November 2026 report quoted BEE as saying trading would begin for most sectors by the end of 2027.

**How it differs from CORSIA:** the CCTS is a **domestic** scheme run by Indian authorities for Indian obligations. CORSIA is an **international** scheme for airlines on international routes, using units from ICAO-approved programmes. ## How Does CORSIA Carbon Credit Trading Work in India?

For an Indian project to supply CORSIA-eligible units, the chain typically looks like this:

1. **Project developer:** designs a project that reduces or removes emissions.
2. **Registration and validation:** the project registers with an **ICAO-approved programme**, such as Verra, Gold Standard or the Global Carbon Council, and is validated by an accredited body.
3. **Monitoring and verification:** results are monitored and independently verified.
4. **Issuance:** the programme issues credits into its **registry**.
5. **CORSIA eligibility assessment:** the credits must fall within the approved scope and vintage for the compliance period.
6. **Host-country authorisation:** India must issue a **Letter of Authorisation (LoA)** for CORSIA use and commit to a **corresponding adjustment**.
7. **Labelling:** the programme registry labels the units as CORSIA eligible.
8. **Buyer identification:** an airline, trader or broker agrees to buy.
9. **Transfer and cancellation:** the units are transferred and cancelled for the airline's compliance in the registry.
10. **Documentation:** supporting records are kept for the airline's report to its national authority.

**A carbon credit generated in India cannot automatically be sold or used as a CORSIA-eligible unit.** Every step must be met.

## Which Carbon Credits Are Eligible for CORSIA Trading?

### Programme-level approval

The ICAO Council approves **emissions unit programmes** against the **CORSIA Emissions Unit Eligibility Criteria**, covering governance, transparency, additionality, permanence, leakage and double counting. Programmes approved for the first phase include the **American Carbon Registry**, **Architecture for REDD+ Transactions**, the **Climate Action Reserve**, the **Global Carbon Council**, **Gold Standard** and **Verra**, among others, each with its own conditions.

### Individual credit eligibility

Programme approval **does not make every credit eligible**. Each unit must also meet the compliance period's conditions:

- **Activity start dates and vintages:** for the first phase, generally activities starting on or after 1 January 2016 and vintages from 2021 to 2026.
- **Methodologies:** some methodologies or project types are excluded within approved programmes.
- **Host-country Letters of Authorisation:** India must authorise the units for CORSIA.
- **Corresponding adjustments:** under **Article 6 of the Paris Agreement**, India adds the authorised tonnes back to its own emissions accounts, so they are not counted twice.
- **Registry labelling and cancellation:** units must be labelled as CORSIA eligible and cancelled through the registry.

**Always check ICAO's latest "CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units" document.** Approvals and conditions are updated, and older lists may be out of date. Our guide to [CORSIA eligible carbon credits](/blog/corsia-eligible-carbon-credits/) explains these checks in more detail.

## Can Indian Carbon Projects Supply CORSIA-Eligible Credits?

**Possibly, but only if every requirement is met.** India's authorisation is the key constraint.

India's government has published a list of activities it would consider for **international transfers under Article 6.2**. It focuses on advanced technologies, such as **renewable energy with storage** (the stored component only), **solar thermal power**, **offshore wind**, **green hydrogen** and **compressed biogas**. Activities not on the list, such as standalone solar PV, are unlikely to be authorised for international use. India has set up a **National Designated Authority for the Implementation of the Paris Agreement** to oversee authorisations. **We have not found a public record of India issuing CORSIA Letters of Authorisation** as of November 2026, so suppliers should check the latest position with the authorities.

By project category:

- **Renewable energy:** many existing Indian projects are older, standalone renewables. They may not be on India's authorisation list, may fail vintage rules, and face stricter programme rules on additionality.
- **Methane reduction:** projects such as landfill gas or biogas could qualify if covered by an approved programme and methodology, and authorised.
- **Clean cooking:** cookstove projects depend on approved methodologies and strong usage monitoring.
- **Waste management:** composting and waste-to-energy projects depend on methodology, additionality and authorisation.
- **Energy efficiency:** industrial projects must be beyond regulatory requirements, and not double-counted under India's domestic schemes.
- **Other activities:** green hydrogen or storage-linked projects align with India's Article 6.2 list, but must still meet programme and ICAO conditions.

**No Indian project type qualifies automatically.**

## Who Can Buy and Sell CORSIA Carbon Credits in India?

- **International airlines:** the end buyers, cancelling units for compliance.
- **Carbon project developers:** the original sellers of issued, eligible units.
- **Carbon credit suppliers and trading companies:** buy and resell units, often aggregating supply.
- **Brokers and intermediaries:** match buyers and sellers for a fee.
- **Carbon market service providers:** consultants, validators, verifiers and registry agents.
- **Institutional buyers and project investors:** finance projects, sometimes through forward offtake agreements.

An **airline buys for compliance** and cancels the units. A **trader or intermediary facilitates transactions** and may hold units temporarily, but cannot use them for compliance itself.

**Not every Indian company can directly trade CORSIA units**, and **ICAO does not run a central exchange**. Trading needs registry accounts with the relevant programmes, eligible inventory and compliant contracts.

## How to Buy CORSIA Carbon Credits in India

1. **Understand the buyer's requirements:** the compliance period, the quantity and the deadline.
2. **Identify the compliance phase:** units must be eligible for that specific phase.
3. **Check ICAO's current eligibility requirements.**
4. **Identify eligible emissions unit programmes** on ICAO's latest list.
5. **Verify each credit's vintage and eligibility**, including the CORSIA label in the registry.
6. **Check host-country authorisation** and corresponding adjustment arrangements.
7. **Carry out project and registry due diligence:** documents, verification reports and serial numbers.
8. **Review supplier documentation**, including the Letter of Authorisation.
9. **Agree commercial and contractual terms**, including price, delivery, replacement guarantees and payment.
10. **Complete the registry transfer and cancellation** as permitted by the programme.

**Verify eligibility before paying.** A credit that later turns out to be ineligible cannot be used for compliance.

## How to Sell CORSIA Carbon Credits from India

1. **Identify eligible inventory:** issued units within the approved programme, scope and vintage.
2. **Verify project documentation:** design document, monitoring and verification reports.
3. **Check registry records:** issuance, ownership and status.
4. **Confirm CORSIA eligibility** against ICAO's latest document.
5. **Obtain host-country authorisation** from India's designated authority.
6. **Prepare due diligence documents** for buyers.
7. **Identify suitable buyers:** airlines, traders or brokers.
8. **Negotiate price and delivery terms.** CORSIA Phase 1 units were assessed at **USD 13.00 per tCO₂e on 18 November 2026** (Fastmarkets), but prices change.
9. **Complete the registry transfer and cancellation** where required.

**Selling ordinary voluntary credits is different.** Voluntary credits need only a buyer willing to accept them. CORSIA units must meet ICAO, programme and host-country requirements first. ## Opportunities for Indian Carbon Credit Developers and Suppliers

- **Aviation-related demand:** IATA estimated first-phase demand at **200–250 million units**, while authorised supply covered about **72 million units** in August 2026.
- **Second-phase demand:** an ICF analysis estimated that about **1.36 billion tonnes** of CO₂ could need offsetting under CORSIA from 2027 to 2035.
- **International buyer relationships** with airlines and traders.
- **High-integrity project development**, especially in activities on India's Article 6.2 list.
- **Carbon finance** for new technologies such as green hydrogen and storage.
- **Project partnerships** with international developers and investors.
- **Services:** validation, verification, registry and advisory work.

**These opportunities depend on actual eligibility, available supply, government authorisation and buyer demand.** None is guaranteed.

## Challenges in CORSIA Carbon Credit Trading in India

- **Strict eligibility requirements** at programme and unit level.
- **Host-country authorisation:** India's list of eligible activities is narrow.
- **Corresponding adjustments:** India must account for authorised units against its own target, which makes authorisation a policy decision.
- **Verification costs** for monitoring and auditing.
- **Supply limitations:** few authorised units exist globally.
- **Registry requirements:** labelling, accounts and cancellation procedures.
- **Regulatory uncertainty:** rules for the second phase and India's authorisation process are still developing.
- **Buyer due diligence:** airlines and traders apply strict checks.
- **International competition** from suppliers in other countries with authorisation frameworks in place.
- **Price volatility:** CORSIA Phase 1 assessments ranged from about USD 9.45 to USD 19.00 during 2026.

## CORSIA Carbon Credits vs India's Domestic Carbon Credit Certificates

| | CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units (EEUs) | Indian Carbon Credit Certificates (CCCs) | Voluntary carbon credits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Regulatory framework** | ICAO rules, with programmes approved by the ICAO Council | Indian CCTS, under BEE, the Ministry of Power and CERC | Independent programme rules |
| **Eligibility** | Approved programme, vintage, LoA, corresponding adjustment, label | BEE-approved methodologies and CCTS rules | Programme methodology |
| **Intended use** | Airline offsetting obligations | Indian compliance targets and domestic offsets | Voluntary corporate claims |
| **Issuance and registry** | Programme registries, such as Verra and Gold Standard | Grid-India registry | Programme registries |
| **Buyers and sellers** | Airlines, traders, international developers | Obligated Indian entities, domestic project owners | Companies, traders |
| **International acceptance** | Accepted for CORSIA if eligible | Domestic, not automatically accepted elsewhere | Varies by buyer |

Other international compliance instruments, such as **EU ETS allowances**, are different again.

**Indian CCCs are not automatically CORSIA eligible** and cannot be treated as interchangeable with CORSIA EEUs.

## Future of CORSIA Carbon Credit Trading in India

**Verified developments:**

- India did **not** volunteer for CORSIA's pilot or first phases, but **Indian airlines will face offsetting requirements from 2027**, under the second phase.
- Indian operators already **monitor and report** emissions under CORSIA, under DGCA requirements.
- India has an **Article 6.2 activity list** and a **National Designated Authority** for authorisations.
- The **CCTS** is being operationalised, with trading expected for most sectors by the end of 2027.

**Possible developments:**

- clearer **authorisation procedures** for CORSIA units
- more Indian projects in **advanced technologies** that fit the authorisation list
- growing **airline demand** as the second phase begins
- continued **supply and integrity challenges** for the global market

This article does not forecast prices or guarantee business opportunities.

## Conclusion

CORSIA carbon credits trading in India is **not an open exchange**. It is a regulated chain: an ICAO-approved programme, an eligible project and vintage, **Indian host-country authorisation with a corresponding adjustment**, registry labelling, and transfer and cancellation for an airline.

India's role is growing, as a **supplier**, a **host country** and, from **2027**, a market whose airlines must offset. But opportunities depend on eligibility, authorisation and demand, and India's domestic **CCCs remain separate** from CORSIA units.
