**CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units** are carbon credits that aeroplane operators can cancel to meet their offsetting requirements under the Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation (CORSIA). Only units that meet all of ICAO's conditions count. The unit must come from a carbon-crediting programme the ICAO Council has approved for the relevant compliance period, and it must fall within that programme's approved scope. It must also meet the date and authorisation rules and be labelled as CORSIA eligible in the programme's registry. For more on how these units are used in aviation, see our [CORSIA Carbon Credits](https://corsiacarboncredit.in/) resources.

Most carbon credits on the market do not meet all of these conditions. This guide explains how ICAO decides eligibility and which criteria matter. It also covers vintage and phase rules, host-country authorisation, how airlines use eligible units, a verification checklist and common mistakes. It is based on ICAO's *CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units* document (April 2026 edition) and TAB recommendations published in September 2026.

## What Are CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units?

An **emissions unit**, usually called a carbon credit, represents one tonne of CO₂ equivalent that has been reduced, avoided or removed from the atmosphere by a specific activity. Examples include a wind farm, a forest protection project or a landfill gas system. A crediting programme issues the unit after the result has been measured and verified.

Under CORSIA, airlines must compensate for the growth in CO₂ from international flights between participating States. They do this by acquiring eligible units and cancelling them, so that each unit is used only once. The number needed depends on the airline's verified emissions and ICAO's growth factors. Our guide to the [CORSIA Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme](/blog/corsia-carbon-offsetting-and-reduction-scheme/) explains how that requirement is set.

The difference from an ordinary carbon credit is **who sets the rules**. For a voluntary credit, the standard and the buyer decide what is acceptable. For a CORSIA eligible emissions unit, ICAO's rules decide, and the airline's State checks that they were followed.

## How Does ICAO Determine CORSIA Emissions Unit Eligibility?

Eligibility is decided in two layers: the **programme** and the **unit**.

### The Technical Advisory Body (TAB)

ICAO's **Technical Advisory Body (TAB)** is a panel of experts set up by the ICAO Council in 2019. It assesses carbon-crediting programmes that apply to supply units to CORSIA, using ICAO's **CORSIA Emissions Unit Eligibility Criteria (EUC)**. The TAB then recommends which programmes, and which parts of them, should be eligible. The **ICAO Council** makes the final decision and publishes it in the ICAO document *CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units*.

### Programme-level eligibility

Approval covers a programme for **specific compliance periods**: the pilot phase (2021–2023), the first phase (2024–2026) or the second phase (2027–2035, assessed period by period). A programme approved for one period is not automatically approved for the next. ICAO also grants some approvals **conditionally**, meaning the programme becomes eligible only after it meets set requirements.

### Unit-level eligibility

Within an approved programme, the ICAO document sets out exactly which units qualify. Conditions can cover:

- **Activity types or methodologies** that are included or excluded
- **Project start dates** and **vintage**, meaning the years in which reductions took place
- **Host-country authorisation** and avoidance of double claiming
- **Registry labelling**: only units held in the listed registry and tagged as CORSIA eligible count

**Programme approval does not make every credit from that programme eligible.** A unit from a well-known, approved standard can still fail on any of these conditions.

## Key Requirements for CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units

The EUC have two parts.

### Carbon offset credit integrity criteria

ICAO's integrity criteria require that units:

- **Are additional**, meaning the reductions would not have happened without the carbon credit incentive
- **Are based on a realistic and credible baseline**
- **Are quantified, monitored, reported and verified**, including independent validation and verification by accredited third parties
- **Have a clear and transparent chain of custody**
- **Represent permanent reductions**, with measures to address the risk of reversal, such as fire destroying a forest project
- **Assess and mitigate leakage**, meaning emissions that simply move elsewhere
- **Are counted only once** towards a mitigation obligation
- **Do no net harm** to people or the environment

### Programme design elements

Programmes must also have sound institutions behind the credits:

- Clear methodologies and a transparent process for developing them
- Issuance, retirement and cancellation procedures
- Registries with unique serial numbers
- Validation and verification rules
- Good governance
- Public participation and transparency
- Environmental and social safeguards
- Sustainable development criteria
- Rules to prevent double issuance, double use and double claiming

### Phase-specific restrictions

ICAO has tightened the rules over time.

- **Pilot phase:** units generally came from activities starting from 1 January 2016, with vintages 2016–2020.
- **First phase:** units must generally come from activities whose first crediting period started on or after 1 January 2016, with vintages from 2021 to 2026. They also need a **host-country Letter of Authorization** and a **corresponding adjustment**, an accounting entry that stops the host country also counting the same reduction towards its own climate target. Our explainer on [corresponding adjustments](/knowledge-base/corresponding-adjustments-made-simple/) covers this in plain terms.

Exact conditions differ by programme, so the ICAO document is always the final reference.

## CORSIA Eligible Carbon Credit Programmes and Standards

A **carbon-crediting programme** (also called a standard) writes the rules for projects. It approves methodologies, sets monitoring and verification requirements, accredits auditors, and runs a registry where each credit gets a unique serial number. ICAO does not issue, sell or hold credits, and it does not run a central exchange. Units are issued and cancelled in the programmes' own registries.

According to ICAO's *CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units* document (April 2026 edition), ten programmes were approved to supply units for the **first phase (2024–2026)**:

- American Carbon Registry (ACR)
- Architecture for REDD+ Transactions (ART)
- BioCarbon Fund Initiative for Sustainable Forest Landscapes (ISFL)
- Climate Action Reserve (CAR)
- Forest Carbon Partnership Facility (FCPF)
- Global Carbon Council (GCC)
- Gold Standard
- Isometric
- Premium T-VER (Thailand)
- Verra's Verified Carbon Standard (VCS)

Each approval comes with its own scope and conditions. Some programmes' units must be cancelled in a specific associated registry to remain eligible.

ICAO has also approved programmes for the **2027–2029 compliance period** and continues to assess more applicants. The Council has conditionally approved the Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism (PACM). ICAO reported in its August–September 2026 newsletter that about 44 million units were labelled CORSIA eligible for the first phase.

Lists change after every Council decision. A programme's reputation or popularity in the voluntary market does not make it CORSIA eligible. **Always check the latest ICAO document** for the compliance period you are buying for.

## Which Types of Emissions Units Can Qualify Under CORSIA?

Where an approved programme's scope allows it, CORSIA eligible units can come from many activity types:

- **Renewable energy projects**, such as wind or solar, where the programme and methodology are approved. Many programmes now restrict grid-connected renewables in countries where they are already commercially viable.
- **Forestry and land-use projects**, including jurisdictional forest protection programmes such as those under ART, FCPF and ISFL, and some reforestation activities.
- **Methane reduction projects**, such as landfill gas capture or methane destruction.
- **Industrial emissions reduction**, such as process improvements, where the methodology is not excluded.
- **Other reduction or removal activities**, including engineered carbon removals under programmes such as Isometric.

**Project category alone does not establish eligibility.** A methane project under an approved programme can still be ineligible if its methodology is excluded, its vintage is outside the window or it lacks host-country authorisation. For example, ICAO's first-phase conditions for Verra exclude certain methodologies, so some Verra credits cannot be labelled for the first phase.

## CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units and Vintage Requirements

**Vintage** is the year in which the emission reduction or removal took place. It matters because:

- ICAO links eligibility to **specific vintage windows** for each compliance period
- older credits may come from methodologies or baselines that no longer meet current standards
- buyers paying for compliance need units their national authority will accept

**Examples of vintage windows:**

- **Pilot phase:** vintages 2016–2020
- **First phase:** vintages 2021–2026

A 2019-vintage credit could have been used for the pilot phase, but **cannot be used for the first phase**. Always check the programme's conditions in the ICAO document for the exact window.

## CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units for Different Compliance Phases

- **Pilot phase (2021–2023):** units from activities starting from 2016, with vintages 2016–2020. No host-country authorisation requirement.
- **First phase (2024–2026):** vintages 2021–2026, with **host-country authorisation** and a **corresponding adjustment** required. Airlines must cancel units for this period by **January 2028**.
- **Second phase (2027–2035):** assessed **period by period**, starting with **2027–2029**. ICAO has already approved some programmes for 2027–2029, and the Council has conditionally approved the Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism.

Conditions can change between phases, and a unit labelled for one phase does not automatically qualify for another. **Rely on the current ICAO document, not older lists or supplier summaries.**

## Host Country Authorization and Corresponding Adjustments

**Host-country authorisation** is a formal approval from the government of the country where a project is located. It allows the project's units to be used for an international purpose, such as CORSIA, usually through a **Letter of Authorization (LoA)**.

A **corresponding adjustment** is an accounting step under **Article 6 of the Paris Agreement**. The host country adds the authorised tonnes back to its own emissions balance, so the same reduction is not counted by both the country and the airline. Without it, one tonne could be claimed twice, which is **double claiming**.

These steps are different from the life cycle of a unit:

- **Authorisation:** the government permits international use.
- **Corresponding adjustment:** the government adjusts its national accounts.
- **Issuance:** the programme creates the unit in its registry.
- **Transfer:** the unit moves between registry accounts.
- **Cancellation:** the unit is permanently used for CORSIA compliance.

For first-phase units, some programmes allow units to be labelled before the corresponding adjustment is confirmed, if an **approved insurance or compensation mechanism** is in place to replace them if the adjustment is never applied. Our guide to [CORSIA carbon credits insurance](/blog/corsia-carbon-credits-insurance/) explains how this works.

## Difference Between CORSIA Eligible Units and Ordinary Voluntary Carbon Credits

| Factor | General carbon credit | CORSIA eligible emissions unit |
|---|---|---|
| Intended use | Voluntary climate claims by companies or individuals | Meeting an airline's CORSIA offsetting requirement |
| Eligibility requirements | Set by the issuing standard and the buyer | Set by ICAO's EUC and Council decisions |
| Programme assessment | No ICAO assessment needed | Programme approved by the ICAO Council on TAB advice |
| Unit-level restrictions | Usually only buyer preferences | Scope, start date, vintage, authorisation and labelling |
| Compliance applicability | Not valid for CORSIA unless every condition is met | Valid only for the compliance period it is eligible for |
| Verification process | Programme verification of the project | Programme verification, plus State review of the airline's cancellation report |

## How Are Eligible Emissions Units Used for CORSIA Compliance?

Airlines meet their offsetting obligations through these steps:

1. **Determine the requirement.** The State calculates the airline's offsetting requirement for the compliance period from its verified emissions on covered routes, after any reductions from CORSIA eligible fuels.
2. **Identify eligible units.** Match potential units against the ICAO document for that period.
3. **Check restrictions.** Confirm the programme, unit type, methodology, vintage, authorisation status and label.
4. **Acquire the units** through a bilateral contract, broker or marketplace.
5. **Transfer** them into the airline's own registry account, or arrange cancellation on its behalf. Transferring a unit changes who holds it, but it can still be used afterwards.
6. **Cancel the units for CORSIA.** Cancellation permanently removes them from circulation for that purpose. In everyday language this is often called retirement. Under CORSIA, the registry entry must state that the units were cancelled for CORSIA, and for which airline and compliance period.
7. **Report.** The airline submits an Emissions Unit Cancellation Report to its State with the serial numbers. The State reviews it and reports to ICAO.

**Buying a carbon credit does not by itself make an airline compliant.** Compliance is shown only when eligible units are cancelled correctly and reported to the State. The national authority checks the cancellation report as part of the wider compliance cycle.

## How to Verify Whether a Carbon Credit Is CORSIA Eligible

Use this checklist before you buy:

- **Check the latest ICAO list.** Confirm the programme is approved for the specific compliance period, and note any conditions.
- **Confirm the programme and activity type.** Make sure the project type and methodology fall inside the approved scope.
- **Verify the vintage and start date.** Both must fall inside the window for that phase.
- **Review the methodology conditions** for any exclusions that apply to it.
- **Check the registry record.** Look up the unique serial numbers and confirm that the units exist, have not been cancelled and carry the CORSIA eligible label.
- **Confirm authorisation.** For first-phase units, check that a Letter of Authorization exists and that the corresponding adjustment conditions apply.
- **Keep evidence.** Store issuance, transfer and cancellation records, which you will need for your report to the State.

Eligibility is always tied to a compliance period. A unit that qualified for the pilot phase is not automatically valid for the first phase. Our [buyer's checklist for CORSIA-eligible units](/knowledge-base/corsia-eligible-units-checklist/) goes through each step in more detail.

## Common Mistakes When Selecting CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units

- **Assuming all voluntary credits are eligible.** Most are not.
- **Confusing programme approval with unit-level eligibility.** An approved programme can still issue ineligible units.
- **Ignoring vintage restrictions**, such as buying pre-2021 credits for the first phase.
- **Overlooking host-country authorisation**, which first-phase units need.
- **Relying on outdated eligibility lists** after new Council decisions.
- **Failing to verify registry records**, such as serial numbers and the CORSIA label.
- **Trusting a supplier's marketing claim.** Only ICAO's rules, the registry record and the national authority's acceptance count.

## Future of CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units

**Confirmed developments:**

- The **second phase begins on 1 January 2027**, with the first compliance period running to 2029.
- ICAO has approved programmes for **2027–2029** and continues to assess new applicants.
- The Council has conditionally approved the **Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism**.

**Market trends and forecasts, not certainties:**

- **Demand** is expected to rise as more States join in 2027. IATA estimated first-phase demand at 200–250 million units, against about 72 million covered by compatible authorisations in August 2026.
- **Article 6 implementation** in more host countries could increase authorised supply.
- **Carbon removals** may become a larger share of eligible units.
- **Registry transparency and traceability** are improving, with clearer labels and authorisation records.

Prices for first-phase units have moved widely, from about US$19 in January 2026 to about US$13 in November 2026 (Fastmarkets). Our guide on [how much a carbon credit is worth](https://corsiacarboncredit.in/blog/value-of-carbon-credits/) compares CORSIA units with other credits.

## Key Takeaways

CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units are what allow international aviation's offsetting system to work. They let airlines compensate for emissions growth with credits that meet a common, ICAO-defined integrity bar.

Eligibility is narrow and changes over time. The programme must be approved for the right compliance period, and each unit must meet its scope, vintage, authorisation and labelling conditions. Before acquiring or using credits, check the current ICAO document, the programme's conditions and the registry records for every serial number.
