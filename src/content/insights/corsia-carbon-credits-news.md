## Introduction to CORSIA Carbon Credits News

**CORSIA**, the Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation, is the scheme adopted by the **International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO)** to address growth in CO₂ from international flights. Airlines meet their offsetting obligations by cancelling **CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units (EEUs)**, carbon credits that meet ICAO's conditions.

News matters because the rules and the market move quickly:

- **ICAO decisions** change which programmes and units are eligible.
- **Host-country authorisations** decide how many units can be labelled.
- **Prices** move with supply and demand.
- **Deadlines** approach: units for 2024–2026 must be cancelled by January 2028.

A decision announced one month can change what an airline can buy the next.

## Latest CORSIA Carbon Credit News and Updates

### October–November 2026: market and industry

- **24 November 2026 (reported):** carbon markets company **Karbon-X announced it had joined IATA's Supporting Alliance for CORSIA Eligible Emissions Unit Supply**, one of a number of companies joining the alliance. *Source: company press release.*
- **18 November 2026 (reported):** **Fastmarkets assessed CORSIA Phase 1 spot units at US$13.00 per tonne**, with bids at US$12.50–12.95. It said more than **55 million tonnes** had been tagged as Phase 1 eligible, still well below forecast demand. *Source: Fastmarkets.*
- **2 November 2026 (reported):** **S&P Global launched separate price assessments for CORSIA Phase 2 units**, reflecting the move towards the second phase. *Source: market reporting on S&P Global.*
- **13 October 2026 (confirmed):** **ICAO joined the IATA-backed Supporting Alliance for CORSIA Eligible Emissions Unit Supply**, which brings together airlines, suppliers and other participants. The alliance aims to help make **225–250 million eligible units available by spring 2027**. *Source: IATA, via EuropaWire and Travel PR News.*

### September 2026: supply and ICAO assessments

- **September 2026 (confirmed):** IATA estimated that airlines could need **200–250 million units** for the first phase, while only about **72 million units** were covered by **42 compatible Letters of Authorisation** from host countries as of August 2026. *Source: IATA.*
- **September 2026 (confirmed):** ICAO's **Technical Advisory Body (TAB)** published recommendations from its **2026 assessment cycle**, covering programmes applying to supply units for the **2027–2029 compliance period**. ICAO also published updated **TAB Procedures (version 9)**. *Source: ICAO TAB documents, September 2026.*
- **August–September 2026 (confirmed):** ICAO's CORSIA newsletter reported that about **44 million units** had been labelled as CORSIA eligible for the first phase, that **four programmes** were approved for 2027–2029 at that point, and that the Council had **conditionally approved the Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism (PACM)** for both the first phase and 2027–2029. *Source: ICAO CORSIA Newsletter, August–September 2026.*

The 72 million figure counts units **covered by government authorisations**, not units already bought and cancelled by airlines. Different sources count labelled supply differently, which explains the gap between ICAO's 44 million and Fastmarkets' 55 million at different dates.

## CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units: Latest Developments

### Approved programmes

ICAO's **April 2026** document lists **ten programmes** for the first phase (2024–2026):

- American Carbon Registry
- Architecture for REDD+ Transactions
- BioCarbon Fund Initiative for Sustainable Forest Landscapes
- Climate Action Reserve
- Forest Carbon Partnership Facility
- Global Carbon Council
- Gold Standard
- Isometric
- Premium T-VER (Thailand)
- Verra's Verified Carbon Standard

For **2027–2029**, a smaller number of programmes had been approved by September 2026, with more under assessment.

### Eligibility requirements

For the first phase, units generally need:

- activities starting on or after **1 January 2016**
- **vintages from 2021 to 2026**
- **host-country authorisation** through a Letter of Authorisation
- a **corresponding adjustment** under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement, so the reduction is not counted twice
- a **CORSIA label** in the programme registry

### Insurance as an alternative safeguard

- **July 2026 (reported):** Gold Standard added **Blenheim**, its first traditional political risk insurer, to its list of approved CORSIA insurance providers, alongside MIGA, Artio, CFC and Oka. *Source: Carbon Herald.*
- **August 2026 (confirmed):** the **Climate Action Reserve** published **criteria for approved insurance mechanisms under CORSIA**. *Source: Climate Action Reserve.*

These policies can stand in where a host country's corresponding adjustment is not yet confirmed. Our guide to [CORSIA carbon credits insurance](/blog/corsia-carbon-credits-insurance/) explains how they work.

### Why programme approval is not enough

**Programme approval does not make every credit eligible.** A credit from an approved programme can still fail on vintage, methodology, authorisation or labelling. Our guide to [CORSIA eligible emissions units](/blog/corsia-eligible-emissions-units/) explains the checks.

## CORSIA Carbon Credit Market Trends

### Demand

Demand is driven by airlines' first-phase obligations and the January 2028 deadline. IATA's estimate of **200–250 million units** remains far above authorised supply.

### Supply

Supply depends mainly on **host-country authorisations**. As of August 2026, **42 Letters of Authorisation** covered about **72 million units** (IATA). The alliance formed in October 2026 aims to close the gap by spring 2027.

### Buyer preferences

Airlines and traders prefer units that are already **labelled**, from **well-known programmes**, with clear **authorisation records** or approved insurance.

### Pricing

*Price assessments, not completed transactions:*

- **28 January 2026:** US$19.00 per tonne (Fastmarkets)
- **1 July 2026:** US$9.45, a Phase 1 low (S&P Global Platts)
- **7 October 2026:** US$12.75 (S&P Global Platts)
- **18 November 2026:** US$13.00 (Fastmarkets)

Prices fell sharply in the first half of 2026 as labelled supply grew, then partly recovered. Our guide to the [CORSIA carbon credit price in 2026](/blog/corsia-carbon-credit-price-2026/) explains what drives these movements.

### Transparency and quality

Registry labels, published Letters of Authorisation, ICAO's eligible units document and price assessments have made the market more transparent. Quality concerns about some project types remain, which is why authorisation and programme conditions matter.

## How CORSIA News Affects Airlines

- **Emissions monitoring and reporting:** these obligations do not change with market news, but airlines must keep reporting accurately each year.
- **Verification and compliance planning:** updated ICAO documents and State pair lists affect which routes are covered and which units count.
- **Carbon credit procurement:** limited supply and price swings encourage early buying, forward contracts and diversified sources.
- **Compliance costs:** a fall from about US$19 to US$13 per unit changes budgets significantly, but prices can rise again as the deadline approaches.
- **Operational decisions:** sustainable aviation fuel and efficiency measures reduce the number of units needed.

## CORSIA Developments and the Global Aviation Industry

- **Airlines:** through IATA, airlines have pressed governments to speed up Letters of Authorisation and joined the October 2026 supply alliance.
- **Governments:** host countries are building Article 6 authorisation processes. Large aviation markets are preparing for mandatory participation. For example, **India**, which did not volunteer for the earlier phases, will face offsetting requirements from 2027, according to an ICCT working paper published in September 2026.
- **Regional rules:** the **European Commission announced a review of the EU Emissions Trading System on 17 July 2026**, and IATA has published a position paper on the review, warning that regional rules should not undermine CORSIA as the global framework.
- **Carbon market participants:** developers, insurers, exchanges and price reporting agencies are adding CORSIA-specific products, such as insurance policies and Phase 2 price assessments.

## Future Outlook for CORSIA Carbon Credits

- **Demand** will rise as more States join in 2027 and the first-phase deadline approaches.
- **Supply** depends on how quickly host countries issue authorisations. The alliance targets 225–250 million units by spring 2027, but that is a goal, not a forecast.

- **Aviation decarbonisation** will increasingly rely on sustainable aviation fuel and efficiency, which reduce the need for offsets over time.

## Conclusion

**CORSIA carbon credits news** in late 2026 centres on one issue: **supply**. Airlines need far more eligible units than host countries have authorised, and ICAO, IATA and market participants are working to close that gap before the January 2028 deadline. Meanwhile, ICAO is preparing the programme list for the **2027–2029** period, prices have settled around **US$13** after a volatile year, and the mandatory **second phase** begins in just over a month.

For airlines and buyers, the practical lessons are to follow official ICAO decisions closely, check every unit's eligibility, and treat market reports as dated snapshots rather than permanent facts.
