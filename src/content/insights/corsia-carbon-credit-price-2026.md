## Introduction to CORSIA Carbon Credit Prices

The **Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation (CORSIA)** is the International Civil Aviation Organization's (ICAO) scheme for addressing CO₂ emissions growth from international flights. Airlines with offsetting requirements meet them by cancelling **CORSIA eligible emissions units**, carbon credits that meet ICAO's rules for the relevant compliance period.

Prices matter to aviation stakeholders because:

- **Airlines** must buy enough eligible units to meet their offsetting requirement, so the price drives their compliance budget.
- **Project developers** need to know what their authorised and labelled units are worth.
- **Traders and financiers** price forward deals and futures against current assessments.
- **Policymakers and analysts** use prices to judge whether supply is keeping up with demand.

Prices vary widely. Two eligible units issued in the same year can sell at different prices because of their project type, documentation or delivery terms. Understanding what drives those differences helps buyers avoid overpaying, or paying for units they cannot use.

## What Are CORSIA Carbon Credits?

**CORSIA carbon credits**, formally CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units (EEUs), are carbon credits that airlines can cancel to meet their CORSIA offsetting requirements. One unit generally represents **one tonne of CO₂ equivalent** reduced or removed.

How they fit into CORSIA:

- **ICAO** sets the rules, including how offsetting requirements are calculated.
- The **ICAO Council**, advised by its Technical Advisory Body (TAB), decides which carbon-crediting programmes are approved for each compliance period, and under what conditions.
- **Programmes** such as Verra, Gold Standard and the Global Carbon Council issue units and label those that are eligible.
- **Airlines** buy the units, cancel them in a registry and report the cancellation to their national authority.

**Not every carbon credit can be used for CORSIA.** For the first phase (2024–2026), units must come from an approved programme, fall within the approved scope and methodologies, have a vintage from 2021 to 2026, come from activities that started on or after 1 January 2016, and carry host-country authorisation with a corresponding adjustment, or an approved compensation arrangement. Ordinary voluntary credits that miss any of these conditions cannot be used, however low their price.

Our guide to [CORSIA eligible emissions units](https://corsiacarboncredit.in/blog/corsia-eligible-emissions-units/) explains the eligibility rules and approved programmes in detail.

## How Much Do CORSIA Carbon Credits Cost?

**In November 2026, first-phase CORSIA eligible units were assessed at around US$13 per tonne of CO₂e.** That is a market reference point, not a fixed price.

Published 2026 price observations for first-phase units include:

- **US$19.00 per tCO₂e, 28 January 2026:** Fastmarkets spot price assessment, reported on 4 February 2026.
- **US$14.50–16.00 per tCO₂e, late January 2026:** traded prices for ICE December 2026 CORSIA futures, as reported by Fastmarkets.
- **US$17.05 bid and US$18.50 offer, 30 January 2026:** spot bid and offer on Climate Impact X, as reported by Fastmarkets. These were not completed trades.
- **US$9.45 per tCO₂e, 1 July 2026:** a first-phase low in S&P Global Platts' price assessment, as reported in market coverage.
- **US$12.75 per tCO₂e, 7 October 2026:** S&P Global Platts price assessment, as reported in market coverage.
- **US$13.15 per tCO₂e, mid-October 2026:** price for December 2026 delivery cited by green.earth, which did not name the assessor.
- **US$13.00 per tCO₂e, 18 November 2026:** Fastmarkets spot price assessment, with bids at US$12.50–12.95.

How to read these figures:

- **A price assessment** is an agency's estimate of where the market is trading on a given day, based on trades, bids and offers. It is not an offer you can buy at.
- **A bid or offer** is what a buyer is willing to pay or a seller is asking. The deal may never happen.
- **A futures price** is for a standard exchange contract with a set delivery date, so it can differ from the spot price on the same day.
- **A broker or supplier quote** is a commercial offer for specific units and depends on volume and terms.

**There is no single CORSIA carbon credit price.** Individual deals can be above or below these assessments. For an explanation of quoting terms, see our page on [how carbon credit prices are quoted](https://corsiacarboncredit.in/knowledge-base/how-carbon-credit-prices-are-quoted/).

## CORSIA Carbon Credit Price in 2026

### Current market pricing

Assessments for first-phase units fell from about US$19 in January to a low of about US$9.45 in July, then recovered to around US$13 by October and November 2026. In November 2026 Fastmarkets reported that buyer inquiries had increased, although overall airline buying remained measured.

S&P Global launched separate price assessments for **second-phase (2027–2029) units** on 2 November 2026, reflecting growing interest in the next compliance period. Second-phase units are a different product and should not be compared directly with first-phase prices.

### Available eligible units

- ICAO's April 2026 eligible emissions units document lists **ten programmes** approved for the first phase.
- ICAO's August–September 2026 newsletter reported about **44 million** labelled units.
- In November 2026, Fastmarkets said more than **55 million tonnes** had been tagged as first-phase eligible.

Figures differ because sources count supply in different ways and at different dates.

### Supply and demand conditions

- **Demand:** in September 2026, IATA estimated that airlines could need **200–250 million units** for the first phase.
- **Authorised supply:** as of August 2026, about **72 million units** were covered by 42 compatible Letters of Authorisation from host countries, according to IATA.
- **Supply response:** on 13 October 2026, ICAO joined the IATA-backed Supporting Alliance for CORSIA Eligible Emissions Unit Supply, which aims to help make **225–250 million units** available by spring 2027. That is a target, not a forecast.

### Airlines' procurement needs and the compliance phase

The first phase covers 2024–2026. States notify airlines of their final offsetting requirements by 30 November 2027, and airlines must cancel units and report by **31 January 2028**. The second phase starts on **1 January 2027**, and its first compliance period runs from 2027 to 2029, with its own approved programmes and conditions.

### Market developments to note

- Newly labelled supply entering the market coincided with falling prices in early 2026.
- Insurance for corresponding adjustment risk is now accepted by some programmes, which can affect deal terms.
- More exchanges and assessments are improving price transparency.

These are actual 2026 developments. What they mean for future prices is uncertain.

## Factors That Affect CORSIA Carbon Credit Prices

### Carbon Credit Programme

Only units from programmes approved for the relevant compliance period count. Programmes differ in their methodologies, registry processes, labelling progress and conditions. Buyers may prefer programmes whose units are already labelled in large volumes, or whose approach to corresponding adjustment risk they trust. Each programme's approval also has conditions, such as excluded methodologies, so programme name alone does not decide eligibility.

### Project Type

Eligible projects include renewable energy, methane capture from landfill and agriculture, efficient cookstoves, forestry, and carbon removals. They differ in:

- **cost of production**, with removals usually more expensive to generate
- **risk**, such as reversal risk for forestry
- **buyer appeal**, as some buyers prefer removals or projects with strong local benefits

Project type alone does not establish eligibility or price. A removal unit with weak documentation can be worth less to a compliance buyer than a well-documented avoidance unit.

### Carbon Credit Vintage

First-phase units need vintages from 2021 to 2026. Units outside that window cannot be used, however cheap they are. Within the window, buyers may prefer more recent vintages, and the eligible vintage window for 2027–2029 differs, so vintage affects both availability and price.

### Host-Country Authorisation and Corresponding Adjustments

First-phase units need a **Letter of Authorisation** from the host country and a **corresponding adjustment**, so the reduction is not also counted towards the host country's own climate target. This affects prices because:

- authorisation is the main bottleneck on supply
- units with completed authorisation and labelling carry less risk for buyers
- forward units still waiting for authorisation carry delivery risk, which usually lowers the price a buyer will pay
- insurance or buffer arrangements add cost and can change contract terms

Authorised units do not automatically cost more in every deal, but authorisation status is one of the biggest influences on what a buyer is willing to pay.

### Market Supply and Demand

Demand estimates for the first phase are far above authorised supply. That gap would normally support prices, but prices fell in early 2026 as new labelled supply arrived and many airlines bought cautiously. Prices respond to news on authorisations, ICAO decisions and airline buying, so they can move quickly in either direction.

### Environmental Integrity and Project Quality

Buyers evaluate:

- **verification** by an accredited independent body
- **additionality**, meaning the project would not have happened without carbon finance
- **permanence**, especially for forestry and removals
- **double-counting prevention**, through authorisation and corresponding adjustments
- **transparency** of project documents and registry records

Stronger integrity can support a higher price and reduce reputational risk, but quality ratings and their effect on price vary between buyers.

### Supplier and Transaction Costs

The price per tonne is only part of what a buyer pays. Other costs include:

- broker commissions or supplier margins
- intermediary fees for structuring or financing deals
- registry account and transfer fees
- due diligence and legal costs
- delivery terms, such as payment on delivery or advance payment
- the cost of insurance or replacement guarantees

## CORSIA Carbon Credit Price vs Voluntary Carbon Credit Price

Different carbon instruments serve different purposes, so their prices are not directly comparable.

- **CORSIA eligible units:** carbon credits that meet ICAO's conditions for a compliance period. Around US$13 per tonne for first-phase units in November 2026 (Fastmarkets).
- **Voluntary carbon credits:** used by companies for their own climate claims. Prices vary hugely by project type. Sylvera reported an average voluntary market price of about **US$6.92 per tonne in Q3 2026**, but high-quality removal credits can sell for far more than CORSIA units.
- **Compliance carbon allowances:** permits to emit under emissions trading systems, such as the EU Emissions Trading System, where allowances traded at about **€90 per tonne in mid-November 2026**, or the UK ETS, at about **£64.40 on 16 November 2026**. Allowances are not carbon credits and cannot be used for CORSIA.
- **Carbon taxes:** prices set by governments per tonne of emissions. They are a policy rate, not a market price. The World Bank's *State and Trends of Carbon Pricing* tracks them.

Why these cannot be compared directly:

- they have different **purposes**, such as compliance versus voluntary claims
- they have different **eligibility and quality rules**
- they are set by different **regulatory conditions**
- they are priced using different **methodologies**, such as assessments, auctions or government rates

**CORSIA eligibility does not automatically create a price premium.** In 2026, first-phase CORSIA assessments were above the average voluntary price reported by Sylvera, but some voluntary credits cost more, and the gap changes with supply and demand.

## How Airlines Calculate Their CORSIA Carbon Credit Costs

An airline's credit cost depends on its **offsetting requirement**, not its total emissions. For the first phase, the requirement is based on the airline's emissions on routes between participating States that are above the baseline of 85% of 2019 emissions, using the sector's growth factor. So an airline does not offset all of its emissions one-for-one.

The basic formula is:

**Estimated credit cost = number of eligible units required × price per unit**

### Illustrative example (hypothetical)

*All values below are hypothetical examples. They are not actual market prices or official CORSIA calculations.*

- Assume an airline needs to acquire **10,000** eligible emissions units.
- Assume a hypothetical purchase price of **US$15** per unit.
- Gross purchase cost: **10,000 × US$15 = US$150,000**.

Additional costs could raise the final figure. For example, if the airline hypothetically paid a 3% brokerage fee, that would add US$4,500. Registry fees, legal review and due diligence would add more. If the airline used units bought earlier at a different price, its average cost would also change.

The actual number of units depends on the airline's verified emissions and the offsetting requirement its State calculates. ## How to Buy CORSIA Eligible Carbon Credits

1. **Identify your compliance requirements:** confirm which routes and years are covered for your airline.
2. **Determine how many units you need:** estimate your offsetting requirement, with a small margin.
3. **Review ICAO's latest eligible programmes:** download ICAO's current eligible emissions units document.
4. **Check vintage and programme conditions:** confirm the vintage window and any excluded methodologies.
5. **Verify authorisation and corresponding adjustments:** ask for the Letter of Authorisation and evidence of how the adjustment is guaranteed.
6. **Identify suppliers:** shortlist developers, suppliers, brokers or platforms.
7. **Compare prices and terms:** compare quotes on the same basis, including delivery date and fees.
8. **Verify registry records:** check serial numbers, ownership and the CORSIA label.
9. **Complete the purchase and transfer:** receive the units in your registry account, or arrange cancellation on your behalf.
10. **Cancel units and keep records:** cancel for CORSIA, report to your State, and keep all documents.

## Where Can Airlines Purchase CORSIA Eligible Carbon Credits?

Airlines can buy through several channels:

- **Direct from project developers:** useful for large volumes and multi-year agreements, but this needs more due diligence.
- **Carbon credit suppliers:** companies that hold inventory from several projects and sell to compliance buyers.
- **Carbon brokers:** intermediaries that match buyers and sellers for a commission.
- **Marketplaces and trading platforms:** as of 27 November 2026, **ICE** lists CORSIA eligible emissions unit futures, **Xpansiv CBL** and **Climate Impact X** have offered CORSIA spot contracts, and **IATA's Aviation Carbon Exchange (ACE)** provides a platform for airlines and sellers.
- **Other intermediaries:** such as banks and trading houses that structure forward deals.

**ICAO does not endorse any supplier, broker or marketplace.** It approves crediting programmes, not sellers. Check each provider's current services and contract terms before trading. Our article on [carbon offset providers and registry management](https://corsiacarboncredit.in/blog/carbon-offset-providers-registry-management-corsia-eligible-credits/) explains how to assess providers.

## How to Check Whether a CORSIA Carbon Credit Is Worth Its Price

Use this checklist before you accept a quote:

- Verify the **programme** against ICAO's current eligibility documentation.
- Check the **specific unit's** eligibility conditions for the compliance period.
- Review the **vintage**.
- Confirm the project **methodology** is not excluded.
- Review **verification and issuance** records.
- Check **registry details** and who owns the units.
- Confirm the **host-country authorisation** and corresponding adjustment arrangements.
- Understand all **supplier fees**.
- Review **delivery and cancellation** terms, including replacement obligations.
- Compare the quote with **similar units** under comparable conditions.

**The lowest-priced credit is not always the best choice.** A cheap unit can carry hidden risks: it may not be labelled yet, its authorisation may be missing, or the contract may give you no remedy if it loses eligibility. An ineligible unit has no compliance value, and replacing it later could cost more than buying a better unit in the first place.

## Why CORSIA Carbon Credit Prices May Change

### Developments that have already moved prices

- New labelled supply entering the market in early 2026, which coincided with lower prices.
- Slow host-country authorisations, which have limited eligible supply.
- The October 2026 supply alliance and growing buyer inquiries in November 2026.

### Possible future influences

- changes to ICAO's eligibility requirements or approved programmes
- changes to programme conditions, such as excluded methodologies
- airline demand, as more States join from 2027
- the pace of new authorisations and labelling
- international climate policy, including Article 6 of the Paris Agreement
- new project availability, especially removals
- registry and market developments, such as new exchanges and assessments
- airline procurement strategies, such as buying early or waiting

None of these guarantees a price rise or fall.

## Common Mistakes When Comparing CORSIA Carbon Credit Prices

- **Comparing CORSIA units with unrelated voluntary credits:** voluntary credits that cannot be used for CORSIA are a different product.
- **Ignoring vintage:** units outside the eligible window have no compliance value.
- **Assuming every credit from an eligible programme qualifies:** methodologies, vintages and labels still matter.
- **Using outdated prices:** first-phase assessments moved by about US$10 per tonne within 2026.
- **Comparing different currencies without conversion:** convert at the same date before comparing.
- **Ignoring transaction costs:** fees can change the real cost per tonne.
- **Confusing quotes with completed trades:** a bid, offer or assessment is not a completed transaction.
- **Assuming similar project types cost the same:** documentation, authorisation and delivery terms differ.
- **Believing CORSIA units always carry a fixed premium:** the gap with voluntary prices changes over time.

## Future Outlook for CORSIA Carbon Credit Prices

Several trends will shape prices from 2027:

- **Supply:** the alliance target of 225–250 million units by spring 2027 depends on host countries issuing authorisations, which has been slow.
- **Demand:** more participating States and the start of the second phase should increase demand, including from airlines in newly participating States such as India.
- **Procurement strategies:** airlines are combining spot purchases with forward contracts, which could make demand more predictable.
- **Regulatory changes:** ICAO Council decisions on programmes for 2027–2029 will shape which units can be used.
- **Market transparency:** separate assessments for first- and second-phase units and more exchange contracts should make prices easier to follow.

Prices could rise if demand outpaces supply, or stay lower if authorised supply grows quickly. This article does not predict future prices or returns.
