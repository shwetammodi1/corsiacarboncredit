*Anonymised and representative: this case study illustrates our approach and does not identify any client, project, price or transaction.*

The buyer's spreadsheet was sorted by price, cheapest first. At the top sat grid-connected solar PV and wind credits, drawn from several registries: Verra VCS, Gold Standard, the Global Carbon Council and ACR. The buyer needed a large volume for a voluntary target and wanted to know which of these it could safely retire. Our brief was data-focused: screen every candidate before any contract, and leave behind an evidence pack that a verifier or auditor could rely on.

## The problem with cheap renewables

Nothing in the offers looked fraudulent. That was never the concern. The concern was **additionality**: whether each reduction happened because of carbon finance.

In many markets, new solar and wind are now the cheapest generation available on their own economics. A plant that would have been built with or without carbon revenue produces credits that represent no extra reduction. That is why several standards have restricted or excluded whole families of renewable energy methodologies, and why the same technology can earn credits in one country and not in another. Indian solar and wind projects registered years ago are a common place for this question to arise.

## The screening funnel

We ran every project through five filters in order, dropping failures at each step so later, slower checks were only spent on survivors.

**Filter 1: Is the methodology still usable?**
Before examining any credit, we checked whether the methodology was active and accepted for the buyer's purpose. Many renewable methodologies are restricted, grandfathered or excluded. A real project on a retired methodology still yields units the buyer cannot use. Some candidates stopped here.

**Filter 2: Does the additionality argument hold?**
For projects that passed, we read the additionality case on its merits: the counterfactual, the financial position at the time of the investment decision, and whether carbon revenue could plausibly have tipped it. Where the project's own economics clearly stood without carbon finance, we flagged it, however low the price.

**Filter 3: Are the units real, current and unused?**
Each unit was checked against the registry of record for issuance, **vintage** and live, un-retired serial numbers. Old vintages offered at a discount with little recent monitoring were treated cautiously.

**Filter 4: Is claimed authorisation genuine?**
Where a seller said a unit carried a **Letter of Authorisation (LoA)** and a **corresponding adjustment** under Article 6, we confirmed it against the published source. For a plain voluntary claim this is optional. For compliance use, including CORSIA, it decides the matter.

**Filter 5: Is the seller clean?**
Running alongside the others: KYC and sanctions screening on each counterparty, so a sound credit was not compromised by who was selling it.

## What came out

| Stage | Typical reason for removal |
|---|---|
| Methodology | Methodology restricted or excluded for the buyer's use |
| Additionality | Project viable on its own economics |
| Registry | Vintage too old, monitoring thin, or serial status unclear |
| Authorisation | LoA or adjustment claimed but not confirmed |
| Counterparty | Identity or sanctions concern |

Many of the cheapest candidates fell out at the first two stages. The buyer went ahead with a smaller subset it could defend and retired those units in its own name, with an evidence pack behind each.

The buyer's summary of the exercise is one we repeat to others: with renewable energy credits, the lowest price on the screen tends to carry the highest risk.

## Where buyers go wrong

- **Checking the registry first.** A clean registry entry says nothing about whether the methodology is still accepted.
- **Assuming a big standard means additional.** Standards have themselves tightened the rules on renewables.
- **Ignoring vintage.** An old unit at a discount may be cheap for a reason.

## Short answers

**Are renewable energy credits always poor?** No, but grid solar and wind often struggle on additionality, so check before relying on them.

**Can they be used for CORSIA?** Only where programme, methodology and unit meet the eligibility criteria, which many renewable methodologies do not.

**Do you take title to credits?** No. We work on screening and verification; the purchase is directly between buyer and seller.
