## Introduction to Carbon Credit Prices in Australia

A **carbon credit** represents a tonne of greenhouse gas emissions reduced or removed. In Australia, the main credit is the **ACCU**, issued by the federal government's **Clean Energy Regulator (CER)**.

Australia's carbon market works like this:

- **Projects** that reduce or store emissions, such as regenerating native forest or capturing landfill gas, earn ACCUs.
- **Buyers** include large industrial facilities covered by the **Safeguard Mechanism**, which must keep emissions under declining limits, as well as the government and voluntary corporate buyers.
- **Trading** happens mainly over the counter through brokers, and ACCUs are held in the **Australian National Registry of Emissions Units (ANREU)**.

Prices matter because they affect project investment, the cost of compliance for heavy industry and the pace of emissions cuts. A **price chart** shows how prices responded to policy changes, supply and demand.

## Understanding Australian Carbon Credit Units (ACCUs)

### What ACCUs are

One ACCU represents **one tonne of CO₂ equivalent (tCO₂e)** stored or avoided by an eligible project. ACCUs are created under the **Carbon Credits (Carbon Farming Initiative) Act 2011**, through what is now called the **ACCU Scheme**. It was long known as part of the **Emissions Reduction Fund**, which also included government purchasing of credits.

### How ACCUs are generated

1. A project registers with the CER under an approved **method**, the scheme's rulebook for each project type.
2. It carries out the activity and monitors results.
3. It submits reports, which are audited where required.
4. The CER **issues** ACCUs into the project's ANREU account.

### The role of the Clean Energy Regulator

The CER registers projects, issues ACCUs, runs the registry, administers the Safeguard Mechanism and publishes the **Quarterly Carbon Market Report**, the main official source of ACCU market data.

### How ACCUs differ from international voluntary credits

- **ACCUs are issued by a government regulator.** Voluntary credits are issued by independent programmes such as Verra or Gold Standard.
- **ACCUs can be used for Australian compliance**, under the Safeguard Mechanism. Most voluntary credits cannot.
- **ACCUs come only from Australian projects.**

### How ACCUs are issued, traded and surrendered

- **Issued** by the CER into the project owner's registry account.
- **Traded** through brokers or directly, and sold to the government under contracts.
- **Surrendered or cancelled**, for example by Safeguard facilities meeting obligations, or by companies making voluntary claims. Once surrendered, they cannot be used again.

### Early development of the market (2012–2020)

The Carbon Farming Initiative began in 2011. After Australia's carbon pricing scheme was repealed in 2014, the government bought ACCUs through **Emissions Reduction Fund auctions** from 2015.

- The first three auctions averaged about **A$11.83 per tonne**.
- Fixed delivery contracts averaged **A$16.32** at the March 2020 auction and **A$15.53** at the September 2020 auction (CER).
- Spot prices stayed in the mid-teens, and RepuTex reported around **A$16.50** in early 2021.

At this stage the government was the main buyer, and auction prices set the tone.

### 2021: A record year

Prices more than tripled in 2021 as **corporate and investor demand** grew:

- From about **A$16.50** at the start of January 2021 (RepuTex)
- To about **A$51** at the start of 2022 (CER)

The CER described 2021 as **another record year** for Australia's carbon markets.

### 2022: Record high and sharp fall

- **Late January 2022:** the generic spot price hit an all-time high of **A$57.50**, on low volumes and some speculative trading (CER).
- **End of February:** about **A$48**.
- **Early March 2022:** the government announced arrangements allowing projects to **exit fixed delivery contracts** with the government and sell their ACCUs on the open market. Prices fell to **A$35.40** on a single high-volume trade, then kept sliding.
- **End of March 2022:** **A$30.50**.
- Integrity concerns about some methods, especially **human-induced regeneration (HIR)**, led to the independent **Chubb Review**.
- **End of 2022:** **A$33.80**.

### 2023: Review and Safeguard reforms

- The government's response to the Chubb Review, released in **January 2023**, found the scheme's arrangements **essentially sound**, while recommending improvements.
- The **reformed Safeguard Mechanism** began on **1 July 2023**, with declining baselines for large emitters. This made **Safeguard facilities the main buyers** of ACCUs.
- Prices were **A$31.85** at the end of June 2023 and **A$33.75** at the end of 2023.

### 2024: Higher compliance demand

- **End of September 2024:** **A$36.25**.
- **19 November 2024:** **A$42.50**, the highest point since 2022 in this series.
- **End of 2024:** **A$36.63**.

### 2025–2026: Stable prices, record supply

- **End of September 2025:** **A$37.43**. **End of 2025:** **A$36.60**.
- **End of March 2026:** **A$36.28**. **End of June 2026:** **A$37.95**.
- **14 August 2026:** **A$39.03**.
- **19 November 2026:** **A$38.05** (CORE Markets).

The CER reported a **record 5.5 million ACCUs issued in Q1 2026**, with 2026 issuance on track for **22 to 26 million ACCUs**. Growing supply has helped keep prices stable despite strong compliance demand.

## Factors Affecting Carbon Credit Prices in Australia

- **Supply and demand:** record issuance in 2026 has balanced rising Safeguard demand.
- **Government climate policies:** the Safeguard Mechanism's declining baselines create compliance demand.
- **ACCU Scheme rules:** new or revised methods change future supply.
- **Project quality and methodology:** integrity concerns can lower demand for some project types.
- **Market confidence:** the 2022 policy announcement showed how quickly confidence can shift.
- **Corporate demand:** voluntary buyers add demand, especially for premium projects.
- **International developments:** global carbon market trends can influence sentiment.
- **Registration and issuance changes:** faster issuance increases available supply.
- **Regulatory uncertainty:** pending reviews and target changes can make buyers cautious.

## Different Types of Australian Carbon Credit Projects

- **Vegetation and reforestation:** includes human-induced regeneration of native forest, environmental plantings and avoided clearing. HIR has been one of the largest sources of ACCUs, and its integrity has been debated.
- **Savanna fire management:** early dry-season burning in northern Australia to reduce later, hotter wildfires. Many of these projects involve **Indigenous ranger groups** and offer co-benefits.
- **Soil carbon:** increasing carbon stored in agricultural soils. Measurement and permanence are challenging.
- **Methane reduction:** for example, capturing methane from piggery or dairy effluent.
- **Landfill gas:** capturing and burning methane from landfills.
- **Energy efficiency:** projects that reduce energy use in industry or buildings.
- **Other eligible activities:** including other methods approved under the scheme.

**Methodology, delivery risk and credit characteristics** affect value. Some buyers pay more for projects with co-benefits or lower perceived integrity risk, and brokers report separate prices for some types, such as HIR and savanna ACCUs. However, **no project type always commands a particular price**. Spreads change with market conditions.

These prices **cannot be compared as interchangeable products**:

- **ACCUs** are government-issued credits usable for Australian compliance.
- **Voluntary credits** are issued by independent programmes and bought by choice.
- **EU allowances** are permits to emit under the EU's cap, not project credits.
- **CORSIA eligible emissions units** must come from programmes approved by ICAO, with host-country authorisation and corresponding adjustments.

**ACCUs are not automatically CORSIA eligible.** For a unit to qualify, its programme must be on ICAO's approved list for the compliance period and the unit must meet unit-level conditions. ## How to Read and Understand an ACCU Price Chart

- **X-axis:** time, shown here from January 2021 to November 2026.
- **Y-axis:** price in **A$ per ACCU**.
- **Daily, monthly and annual movements:** daily charts show volatility; quarterly points, like those in the cover chart, show the trend.
- **Historical highs and lows:** the A$57.50 record in January 2022 and the fall to about A$30 in March 2022 stand out.
- **Volatility:** sharp moves often follow policy announcements.
- **Trading volume:** where available, low volumes can make prices less representative. The January 2022 peak came on low volumes.
- **Spot versus forward prices:** spot prices are for immediate delivery, while forward prices are for future delivery and may differ.
- **Interpretation:** a chart shows what happened, not what will happen.

## Why Do Australian Carbon Credit Prices Change?

- **Policy announcements:** after the March 2022 contract exit announcement, prices fell by more than a third within weeks, from about A$48 to A$30.50.
- **Supply conditions:** record issuance in 2026 has kept prices steady.
- **Demand changes:** the 2023 Safeguard reforms made heavy industry the main buyer.
- **Regulatory developments:** reviews of methods, such as the Chubb Review and the Climate Change Authority's periodic ACCU Scheme reviews, affect confidence.
- **Speculation and low volumes:** these contributed to the January 2022 peak.

## Future Outlook for Australian Carbon Credit Prices

**Confirmed policy settings:**

- Australia's targets are a **43% cut below 2005 levels by 2030** and **net zero by 2050**, and in 2025 the government set a **2035 target of 62–70%** below 2005 levels.
- Safeguard baselines decline each year, which supports compliance demand.
- Safeguard facilities account for **around 80% of ACCU demand**, according to the Climate Change Authority's 2026 ACCU Scheme review.

**Uncertainties:**

- **Supply growth**, with 22 to 26 million ACCUs expected to be issued in 2026, could weigh on prices.
- **Regulatory reforms**, such as method changes or adjustments to Safeguard rules.
- **Corporate demand**, which depends on climate commitments and economic conditions.

Prices could rise or fall. **This article does not forecast or guarantee future prices.**

- **Clean Energy Regulator:** Quarterly Carbon Market Reports and data workbooks, with spot prices, issuance and demand.
- **Australian Government publications:** the Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water and the Climate Change Authority.
- **Broker market reports:** such as CORE Markets and Jarden, which publish prices and commentary.

The Australia carbon credit price chart tells a clear story: ACCUs rose from the mid-teens to a **record A$57.50 in January 2022**, fell to about A$30 after a policy change, and have since traded mostly in the **A$30–43 range**, closing at **A$38.05 on 19 November 2026**.
