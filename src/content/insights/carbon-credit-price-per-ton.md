**What is the price of carbon credits per ton?** There is no single price. In 2026:

- the **average voluntary carbon credit** retired in Q3 2026 sold for about **USD 6.92 per tonne of CO₂e**, according to Sylvera data
- **CORSIA-eligible units** for international aviation were assessed at roughly **USD 13–19 per tonne**
- some **engineered carbon removals**, such as biochar, have sold for **over USD 160 per tonne**

What you pay depends on project type, quality, vintage, eligibility and market conditions. For aviation-specific pricing, see our [CORSIA Carbon Credits](https://corsiacarboncredit.in/) resources.

This guide explains each price driver, gives dated figures and shows how to estimate a purchase budget. All figures reflect sources available in November 2026.

## What Is a Carbon Credit and What Does One Ton Represent?

A **carbon credit** is a certificate issued by a crediting programme. It generally represents **one metric tonne of carbon dioxide equivalent (tCO₂e)** that a project has reduced, avoided or removed, under the issuing programme's rules. **CO₂e** converts other gases, such as methane, into the amount of CO₂ with the same warming effect.

A **carbon offset** is the **use** of a credit. When a buyer retires a credit against its own emissions, the credit becomes an offset. Our explainer on the [difference between carbon credits and carbon offsets](/blog/difference-between-carbon-credit-and-carbon-offset/) covers this in detail.

Quality and verification matter because a credit is only worth paying for if the tonne behind it is real. That means the project must:

- be **additional**, meaning it would not have happened anyway
- be measured against a realistic **baseline**
- be **independently verified**
- be tracked in a **registry** so the credit is used only once

The figures are of different types:

**EU ETS allowances** are permits to emit under a cap, not carbon credits. Voluntary credits cannot be used in their place, so the two prices are not directly comparable.

## What Factors Affect the Price of Carbon Credits?

- **Project type.** Reductions, such as renewable energy, usually cost less than removals, such as biochar or direct air capture.
- **Environmental integrity and additionality.** Credits with strong evidence that the project needed carbon finance tend to command more.
- **Verification and certification standards.** The programme and methodology version affect buyer confidence.
- **Permanence and reversal risk.** Durable storage is valued more highly. Forestry credits carry fire and logging risk, which buffer pools help manage.
- **Vintage and location.** Newer vintages are often preferred, and some buyers or rules favour certain regions or require host-country authorisation.
- **Co-benefits.** Projects with biodiversity, health or community benefits can earn a premium.
- **Supply and demand.** Limited supply of a credit type, or rising demand for it, pushes prices up.
- **Regulations and buyer requirements.** Eligibility for compliance schemes such as CORSIA narrows supply and raises value.

## Voluntary Carbon Market vs Compliance Carbon Market Pricing

- **Voluntary carbon market:** companies buy credits from programmes such as Verra or Gold Standard for their own climate goals. Prices depend on project type, quality ratings and how many companies are buying.
- **Compliance carbon markets:** governments or international bodies require regulated entities to hold allowances or eligible units. Legal obligations create steady demand, and supply is set by the scheme. In the EU ETS, for example, that has kept allowance prices far above average voluntary credit prices.

Prices across markets **cannot be compared directly**, because the instruments, rules and uses differ. A voluntary credit is **not automatically eligible** for any compliance scheme.

## What Is the Price of CORSIA Carbon Credits Per Ton?

**CORSIA**, the Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation, is ICAO's global market-based measure for international aviation's CO₂. Airlines with offsetting requirements on routes between participating States must cancel **CORSIA eligible emissions units**.

There is **no official CORSIA price**. ICAO decides which units are eligible, but it does not sell credits or run an exchange. Published assessments for first-phase (2024–2026) units fell from **USD 19.00 per tonne on 28 January 2026** (Fastmarkets spot assessment) to **USD 13.15 per tonne** for December 2026 delivery (cited in mid-October 2026). The fall followed more labelled units reaching the market.

What affects the price of a CORSIA unit:

- whether the **programme** is approved for the relevant compliance period
- whether the **unit** meets the conditions: for the first phase, activities starting on or after 1 January 2016, vintages from 2021 to 2026, host-country authorisation and a corresponding adjustment, and a CORSIA label
- the balance between limited eligible **supply** and airline **demand**

**Ordinary voluntary credits are not automatically CORSIA eligible.** Our guide to the [CORSIA carbon credit price in 2026](/blog/corsia-carbon-credit-price-2026/) covers these figures in more depth.

## How Can Businesses Estimate the Cost of Buying Carbon Credits?

> **Total estimated cost = Number of carbon credits × Price per credit**

Then add brokerage, registry and due diligence costs.

### Example 1: Voluntary climate commitment (hypothetical)

*All figures are illustrative assumptions, not market quotes.* A company wants to offset 1,000 tonnes of residual emissions and assumes **USD 10 per credit**:

1,000 × USD 10 = **USD 10,000**, before fees.

### Example 2: Comparing nature-based and engineered removals (hypothetical)

*Illustrative assumptions only.* An organisation wants 500 tonnes of removals and compares:

- **Reforestation credits** assumed at USD 20: 500 × 20 = **USD 10,000**
- **Biochar credits** assumed at USD 150: 500 × 150 = **USD 75,000**

The higher cost buys more durable storage, but the organisation also weighs co-benefits and its budget.

### Example 3: An airline under CORSIA (hypothetical)

*Illustrative assumptions only.* An airline's State confirms an offsetting requirement of 20,000 tonnes for the compliance period. The airline assumes **USD 15** per eligible unit:

20,000 × USD 15 = **USD 300,000**, before fees.

It must also confirm that each unit is eligible and cancel the units for CORSIA. Our article on [CORSIA eligible carbon credits](/blog/corsia-eligible-carbon-credits/) explains the eligibility checks.

## How to Evaluate Whether a Carbon Credit Is Worth Its Price

- **Project documentation:** read the design document and monitoring reports.
- **Independent validation and verification:** review the auditor's reports.
- **Registry records:** confirm that the serial numbers exist and the credits are active.
- **Additionality and permanence:** look at the evidence, and at buffer pools for nature-based credits.
- **Double-counting safeguards:** check that no one else can claim the same tonne.
- **Retirement records:** make sure you will receive proof that the credits were retired in your name.
- **Host-country authorisation and corresponding adjustments**, where your use requires them.
- **CORSIA eligibility** against ICAO's current document, if the credits are for aviation compliance.

**The cheapest credit is not always the best value.** A low-priced credit that fails these checks, or does not qualify for your purpose, can cost more in the end through reputational or compliance risk. A high price does not guarantee quality either.

## How Could Carbon Credit Prices Change in the Future?

Possible influences include:

- **climate regulations and policy**, including rules on environmental claims and new compliance schemes
- **corporate net-zero commitments**, which affect voluntary demand
- **aviation compliance demand**, as CORSIA's second phase starts in 2027
- **carbon removal technology**, where costs may fall as projects scale
- **quality standards and integrity initiatives**, which are already widening the price gap between low- and high-rated credits
- **supply and buyer preferences**, such as new issuances, methodology changes and interest in removals

These are possibilities, not predictions. Carbon credit prices have moved substantially in both directions in recent years, and future prices cannot be forecast with certainty.
