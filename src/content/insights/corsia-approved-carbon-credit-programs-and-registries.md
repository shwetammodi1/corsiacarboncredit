**What are CORSIA approved carbon credit programs and registries?** CORSIA approved programmes are carbon-crediting programmes, such as Verra, Gold Standard and the Global Carbon Council, that the **ICAO Council** has approved to supply eligible emissions units for a CORSIA compliance period. Each programme runs, or uses, a **registry** that records the units it issues, who owns them and when they are cancelled. ICAO's April 2026 document lists **ten programmes** approved for the First Phase (2024–2026). This guide from [CORSIA Carbon Credits](https://corsiacarboncredit.in/) explains how programmes are approved, how registries work and how to verify a unit.

Information in this guide was checked on **27 November 2026**. ICAO updates its list after Council decisions, so always check the latest version of its CORSIA eligible emissions units document before buying.

Programme approval is one of the core requirements of CORSIA's First Phase. Our guide to [CORSIA Phase 1 requirements](https://corsiacarboncredit.in/blog/corsia-phase-1-requirements/) explains how it fits with monitoring, reporting and offsetting.

## Introduction to CORSIA Approved Carbon Credit Programs

**CORSIA**, the Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation, is ICAO's scheme for addressing growth in CO₂ emissions from international flights. Airlines with offsetting requirements meet them by cancelling **CORSIA eligible emissions units**, carbon credits that represent one tonne of CO₂ equivalent reduced or removed elsewhere.

Carbon credits are used because the aviation sector cannot yet cut all its emissions growth through new aircraft, operations and sustainable fuels. Offsetting fills the gap while those measures develop.

Two systems make this work:

- **Programme approval** ensures that units come from programmes with strong rules on quantification, verification and double counting.
- **Registries** ensure that each unit is tracked from issuance to cancellation, so it can only be used once.

Neither is enough on its own. An airline must check that a unit comes from an approved programme, meets that programme's CORSIA conditions, and is correctly recorded and labelled in the registry, **before** it buys.

## What Are CORSIA Approved Carbon Credit Programs?

An **emissions unit programme** is an organisation that sets the rules for creating carbon credits and issues them. Programmes:

- **set standards and methodologies**, the rules for measuring emission reductions or removals for each project type
- **register projects** that meet their requirements
- **require independent validation**, where an accredited body checks the project design before it starts
- **require independent verification**, where an accredited body checks the reductions or removals actually achieved
- **issue credits** for verified results into their registry

Projects generate credits by reducing emissions compared with a baseline, such as capturing methane at a landfill, or by removing CO₂ from the atmosphere, such as through reforestation or direct air capture.

For CORSIA, ICAO assesses whole programmes against its **Emissions Unit Eligibility Criteria**. If approved, a programme can supply eligible units, but only within the scope and conditions ICAO sets.

**Programme approval does not make every credit eligible.** ICAO may exclude certain methodologies, project types or vintages, and units must also meet the compliance period's authorisation and labelling rules.

## How Does ICAO Approve Carbon Credit Programs for CORSIA?

The process usually follows these steps:

1. **Application:** a programme responds to ICAO's call for applications and submits evidence of how it meets the criteria.
2. **TAB assessment:** the ICAO **Technical Advisory Body (TAB)**, a group of experts nominated by States and observer organisations, reviews the application.
3. **Evaluation against the criteria:** the TAB checks the programme's rules against the Emissions Unit Eligibility Criteria and its own procedures. The current version, TAB Procedures v9, was published in September 2026.
4. **Recommendation:** the TAB recommends to the ICAO Council whether to approve the programme, for which compliance period, and with what scope and conditions.
5. **Council decision:** the ICAO Council considers the recommendations and decides.
6. **Publication:** approved programmes and their conditions are published in ICAO's **CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units** document.

### Assessment principles

ICAO's criteria look at whether a programme's rules deliver:

- **Environmental integrity:** credits represent real reductions or removals.
- **Additionality:** reductions would not have happened without carbon finance.
- **Accurate quantification:** baselines and measurements are conservative and robust.
- **Independent verification:** accredited third parties check results.
- **Permanence:** reversal risks, for example from forest fires, are managed, such as through buffer pools.
- **Avoidance of double counting:** the same reduction is not issued, used or claimed twice.
- **Transparency:** project documents and registry records are publicly available.
- **Sustainable development and safeguards:** projects avoid social and environmental harm.

## List of CORSIA Approved Carbon Credit Programs in 2026

ICAO's April 2026 CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units document lists the following ten programmes for the **First Phase (2024–2026)**. For all of them, First Phase units must have a **vintage from 2021 to 2026**, come from activities that started on or after **1 January 2016**, have **host-country authorisation with a corresponding adjustment** or an approved compensation arrangement, and carry the programme's **CORSIA First Phase label**.

### American Carbon Registry (ACR)

- **What it is:** a US-based programme founded in 1996 and run by Winrock International.
- **Framework:** issues Emission Reduction Tonnes for projects such as landfill gas, forestry and industrial process changes, using its own approved methodologies.
- **Registry:** the ACR Registry.
- **ICAO status:** approved for the First Phase, within the scope set by ICAO.

### Architecture for REDD+ Transactions (ART)

- **What it is:** a programme for **jurisdictional** forest protection, where national or large subnational governments reduce deforestation across a whole area.
- **Framework:** issues credits under its **TREES** standard.
- **Registry:** the ART Registry.
- **ICAO status:** approved for the First Phase, within the scope set by ICAO.

### BioCarbon Fund Initiative for Sustainable Forest Landscapes (ISFL)

- **What it is:** a World Bank-managed fund that pays for emission reductions from jurisdiction-wide land-use programmes.
- **Framework:** issues emission reductions under its own requirements for jurisdictional programmes.
- **Registry:** units are tracked in the World Bank's registry system.
- **ICAO status:** approved for the First Phase, within the scope set by ICAO.

### Climate Action Reserve (CAR)

- **What it is:** a programme with protocols mainly for projects in the United States and Mexico.
- **Framework:** uses standardised **protocols** and issues Climate Reserve Tonnes.
- **Registry:** the Reserve's own registry.
- **ICAO status:** approved for the First Phase. CAR published criteria for approved insurance mechanisms for CORSIA in August 2026.

### Forest Carbon Partnership Facility (FCPF)

- **What it is:** a World Bank partnership that supports jurisdictional REDD+ programmes in developing countries.
- **Framework:** its Carbon Fund pays for verified emission reductions at a jurisdictional scale.
- **Registry:** units are tracked in the World Bank's registry system.
- **ICAO status:** approved for the First Phase, within the scope set by ICAO.

### Global Carbon Council (GCC)

- **What it is:** a programme based in Qatar, serving the Middle East, Asia and Africa, with many projects in South Asia, including India.
- **Framework:** issues Approved Carbon Credits, using its own and adapted methodologies.
- **Registry:** the GCC Registry.
- **ICAO status:** approved for the First Phase, within the scope set by ICAO.

### Gold Standard

- **What it is:** a Geneva-based programme founded in 2003 by WWF and other NGOs.
- **Framework:** issues Verified Emission Reductions and requires projects to show **sustainable development benefits**.
- **Registry:** the Gold Standard Impact Registry.
- **ICAO status:** approved for the First Phase. Gold Standard accepts approved insurers to cover corresponding adjustment risk; its approved list included MIGA, Artio, CFC, Oka and Blenheim as of July 2026.

### Isometric

- **What it is:** a newer programme focused on **carbon removal**, such as enhanced weathering and biochar.
- **Framework:** issues removal credits under its own protocols, with an emphasis on scientific review.
- **Registry:** the Isometric Registry.
- **ICAO status:** approved for the First Phase, within the scope set by ICAO.

### Premium T-VER (Thailand)

- **What it is:** the premium tier of Thailand's voluntary emission reduction programme, run by the Thailand Greenhouse Gas Management Organization (TGO).
- **Framework:** applies stricter requirements than the standard T-VER tier.
- **Registry:** a registry operated by TGO.
- **ICAO status:** approved for the First Phase, within the scope set by ICAO.

### Verra's Verified Carbon Standard (VCS)

- **What it is:** the largest crediting programme by volume of credits issued.
- **Framework:** issues Verified Carbon Units (VCUs) across many project types.
- **Registry:** the Verra Registry.
- **ICAO status:** approved for the First Phase, with exclusions. Under ICAO's conditions, methodologies including **VMR0006** and **AMS-II.G** are excluded for the First Phase.

### Conditional approvals and other compliance periods

- **Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism (PACM):** the UN mechanism under Article 6.4 has **conditional** approval for the First Phase and for 2027–2029. Conditional means some requirements must still be met before its units can be used.
- **2027–2029:** ICAO's August–September 2026 newsletter reported that **four programmes** had been approved for the first compliance period of the Second Phase. Check ICAO's latest document for their names and conditions.
- **Pending decisions:** the TAB made new recommendations in September 2026, and the ICAO Council is expected to consider them. **Recommendations are not approvals** until the Council decides.
- **Pilot Phase (2021–2023):** a different list of programmes applied, with vintages from 2016 to 2020. Pilot Phase approvals do not carry over automatically.

## What Are Carbon Credit Registries?

A **carbon credit registry** is a database that tracks carbon credits throughout their life. It provides:

- **Project registration:** a public record of each project, with its documents.
- **Credit issuance:** a record of each batch of credits issued after verification.
- **Unique serial numbers:** each credit, or block of credits, has a unique serial number showing the project, vintage and issuance.
- **Ownership records:** account holders and the credits they hold.
- **Transfers:** a record of credits moving between accounts.
- **Retirement or cancellation:** a permanent record when a credit is used, so it cannot be sold again.
- **Double-counting prevention:** each serial number can only be cancelled once.
- **Public transparency:** most registries publish issuance and cancellation data, so anyone can trace a credit.

### Programme vs registry

A **programme** sets the rules and decides which credits to issue. A **registry** is the system that records them. Many programmes run their own registry, such as the Verra Registry, but some use registries run by other organisations. Our knowledge-base page on [carbon registries and serial numbers](https://corsiacarboncredit.in/knowledge-base/carbon-registries-and-serial-numbers/) explains how serial numbers are structured.

## How Do Carbon Credit Registries Work Under CORSIA?

The typical life of a CORSIA eligible unit:

1. **Registration:** a project is registered under an approved programme.
2. **Methodology:** the project follows an approved methodology within ICAO's scope.
3. **Monitoring:** the project monitors its emission reductions or removals.
4. **Verification:** an accredited verification body checks the results.
5. **Issuance:** the programme issues credits for the verified amount.
6. **Recording and labelling:** units are recorded in the registry and, once authorisation conditions are met, labelled as CORSIA eligible for the relevant period.
7. **Transfer:** units can be sold and transferred to traders and airlines.
8. **Cancellation:** the airline cancels the units for CORSIA, naming itself and the compliance period, and reports the cancellation to its State.

Registry records matter because the serial number is the only reliable way to prove that a specific unit exists, meets the label requirements, belongs to the seller and has not already been used. Airlines report serial numbers in their Emissions Unit Cancellation Report.

## Difference Between CORSIA Approved Programs and Carbon Registries

**Carbon credit programmes:**

- establish standards and methodologies
- define project requirements
- set monitoring and verification rules
- oversee credit issuance under their framework

**Carbon registries:**

- record issued emissions units
- track ownership and transfers
- maintain serial numbers and unit records
- record cancellation or retirement

**A registry listing does not establish CORSIA eligibility.** A credit can be in an approved programme's registry and still be ineligible, for example because of its vintage or methodology, or because it lacks authorisation. Eligibility comes from ICAO's approval and conditions, shown through the programme's CORSIA label.

## ICAO CORSIA Central Registry (CCR)

The **CORSIA Central Registry (CCR)** is an ICAO system for publishing CORSIA implementation information. It is **not** a carbon credit registry or marketplace.

The CCR includes:

- **State information:** participating States and national authority contacts.
- **Aeroplane operator information:** which operators are attributed to which State.
- **Emissions data:** aggregated CO₂ emissions reported by States.
- **Compliance information:** information on eligible fuel claims and emissions unit cancellations reported by States.

How it differs from programme registries:

- **It does not issue, hold, transfer or sell carbon credits.** Units are issued and cancelled in the programmes' registries.
- **It records information reported by States**, not individual airline trades.
- **ICAO does not run a commercial exchange.**

## How to Verify a CORSIA Approved Programme and Carbon Credit

Use this checklist for every unit you consider buying:

- **Check ICAO's latest eligible emissions units document**, and note its date.
- **Confirm the programme's approval status** for the relevant compliance period.
- **Identify the compliance period**, such as 2024–2026 or 2027–2029.
- **Review the programme's specific conditions**, including exclusions.
- **Check the project type and methodology** against those conditions.
- **Verify the vintage** falls within the period's window.
- **Review issuance and serial number details** in the registry.
- **Confirm the registry record**, including the current owner and status.
- **Check the host-country Letter of Authorisation.**
- **Review how the corresponding adjustment is guaranteed**, such as through insurance or a buffer.
- **Confirm the specific unit carries the CORSIA label** for the period.

**Never rely only on a supplier's claim that a credit is CORSIA eligible.** Check the evidence yourself. Our article on [carbon offset providers and registry management](https://corsiacarboncredit.in/blog/carbon-offset-providers-registry-management-corsia-eligible-credits/) explains how to assess providers and registry records.

## Role of Host-Country Authorisation and Corresponding Adjustments

- **Host-country authorisation:** the government of the country where the project is located formally approves the use of its units for international purposes, such as CORSIA.
- **Letter of Authorisation (LoA):** the document that records this approval.
- **Corresponding adjustment:** an accounting entry in which the host country adds the transferred reductions back to its own emissions balance under the Paris Agreement, so it does not also count them towards its national target.
- **Double claiming:** without an adjustment, both the host country and the airline could claim the same reduction. CORSIA's First Phase rules require units to avoid this.
- **Article 6:** these rules link CORSIA to Article 6 of the Paris Agreement, which governs international transfers of mitigation outcomes.
- **Unit-specific documents:** buyers should check the LoA and adjustment arrangements for each project, because they differ between countries and programmes.

Our knowledge-base page on [corresponding adjustments made simple](https://corsiacarboncredit.in/knowledge-base/corresponding-adjustments-made-simple/) explains the accounting in plain terms.

## Why Approved Programs and Registries Matter to Airlines

- **Compliance confidence:** only approved, eligible units meet the offsetting requirement.
- **Environmental integrity:** ICAO's criteria aim to ensure each unit represents a real tonne.
- **Traceability:** serial numbers show where each unit came from.
- **Transparent ownership:** registry records show who holds a unit before you pay.
- **Reliable procurement:** clear rules make it easier to compare suppliers.
- **Reduced double counting:** authorisation and single cancellation protect against double claims.
- **Verification:** labels and records let airlines and States confirm eligibility.
- **Recordkeeping:** cancellation records support the airline's report to its State.

## Common Mistakes When Selecting CORSIA Carbon Credit Programs and Registries

- **Assuming programme approval means all units qualify:** exclusions, vintages and labels still apply.
- **Confusing programme approval with registry registration:** a registry entry is not proof of eligibility.
- **Ignoring vintage restrictions:** only 2021–2026 vintages count for the First Phase.
- **Using outdated ICAO lists:** the list changes after Council decisions.
- **Confusing the CCR with carbon credit registries:** the CCR does not hold or cancel units.
- **Not verifying serial numbers:** without them, you cannot prove a unit exists or is unused.
- **Overlooking authorisation:** First Phase units need an LoA and a corresponding adjustment.
- **Assuming any voluntary credit can be used:** most voluntary credits do not meet CORSIA conditions.

## Future Developments in CORSIA Approved Programs and Registries

### Confirmed developments

- The TAB published updated procedures (v9) and new recommendations in September 2026.
- PACM has conditional approval for the First Phase and 2027–2029.
- ICAO joined the IATA-backed supply alliance in October 2026, which aims to help make 225–250 million units available by spring 2027.

### Pending or expected

- **Council decisions** on the September 2026 TAB recommendations, expected but not confirmed.
- **New programme applications** in future TAB assessment cycles.
- **More approvals for 2027–2029**, as more programmes are assessed.

### Possible longer-term trends

- **Registry transparency:** more detailed public data on labels and authorisations.
- **Tracking technology:** better links between registries to prevent double use.
- **Digital verification:** faster checks of serial numbers and labels.
- **Article 6 cooperation:** more host-country authorisations as national systems develop.
- **Supply:** more eligible units if authorisations speed up.
- **Market participation:** more airlines buying as more States join the Second Phase.

These trends are expectations, not ICAO decisions.
