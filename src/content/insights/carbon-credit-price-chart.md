**A carbon credit price chart** shows what carbon credits have sold for over time, usually in US dollars per tonne of CO₂ equivalent (USD/tCO₂e). On Ecosystem Marketplace data, the **voluntary market's average price** rose from **USD 2.51 in 2020 to USD 7.37 in 2022**, then eased to **USD 6.34 in 2024**. In 2026:

- the average retired voluntary credit sold for about **USD 6.92** (Q3, Sylvera)
- CORSIA first-phase units were assessed at about **USD 13**
- EU ETS allowances, a different instrument, traded near **€90**

For aviation-specific pricing, see our [CORSIA Carbon Credits](https://corsiacarboncredit.in/) resources.

Every chart below uses sourced figures, shows its source and date, and leaves out years where comparable data does not exist. Research was done in November 2026.

## What Is a Carbon Credit Price Chart?

A **carbon credit** represents one tonne of CO₂e reduced or removed by a verified project. A **price chart** plots what buyers paid, or what analysts assessed, over a period.

Prices are given **per tonne of CO₂e** because that is the unit credits are issued in. Charts help:

- **buyers** budget and judge whether a quote is reasonable
- **sellers** price their credits
- **investors and researchers** follow market trends

Two cautions apply to any chart:

- **Credits are not allowances.** Allowances are government permits to emit under a cap, such as in the EU ETS. They are traded in compliance markets, often in euros, and their prices are far higher. They are not the same product.
- **There is no single universal price.** Prices differ by project category, methodology, vintage, quality, programme, market segment and type of transaction. A chart of averages can hide a wide spread.

## Global Carbon Credit Price Chart and Historical Trends

![Line chart of voluntary carbon market average prices from 2019 to 2024 in USD per tonne: $3.07, $2.51, $4.04, $7.37, $6.53 and $6.34](/media/carbon-price-chart-history.png)

*Source: Ecosystem Marketplace, State of the Voluntary Carbon Market reports (2021–2025 editions). Volume-weighted annual averages of reported voluntary transactions, as first reported. Later editions revise some years slightly. No comparable annual figure for 2025–2026 was published in this series at the time of writing.*

What the history shows:

- **2019–2020:** a low-price market dominated by cheap renewable energy and older credits, averaging around USD 2.50–3.
- **2021–2022:** a surge in corporate net-zero pledges lifted demand. Average prices nearly doubled, from USD 4.04 to USD 7.37.
- **2023–2024:** media and research scrutiny of credit quality, especially some forest credits, reduced confidence. Volumes fell and averages eased to the mid-USD 6 range.
- **2026:** a different dataset (Sylvera, averages of credits retired) shows about USD 6.12 for Q1–Q3 2026 and USD 6.92 for Q3 alone. That is broadly similar to 2024, but the two series should not be joined into one line.

These are **market-wide averages**. Individual project prices can be far above or below them. The shifts reflect changes in supply and demand, regulation, buyer preference for quality, and market confidence.

How to read quoted prices:

- **Offers** are asking prices. **Completed transactions** are what was actually paid, and the two can differ.
- **Spot prices** are for immediate delivery. **Long-term purchase agreements** fix prices for future delivery and include delivery risk.
- Prices **vary between suppliers and projects**, even within one category.
- Always check the **date, currency, unit and type of figure** before comparing.

## Carbon Credit Prices by Project Type

![Bar chart of 2024 voluntary credit prices by category: renewable energy $2.67, all reduction credits $4.05, household devices $7.30, all removal credits $19.50, biochar over $160](/media/carbon-price-chart-by-category.png)

*Source: Ecosystem Marketplace, State of the Voluntary Carbon Market 2025 (2024 data), as compiled by a public price tracker reviewed on 5 October 2026. All bars use the same dataset, year and unit.*

### 1. Renewable energy

Solar and wind credits are plentiful and often from older projects. In many markets renewables no longer need carbon finance to be built, which weakens their additionality. The result is low prices.

### 2. Forestry and REDD+

There are two main types:

- **Forest conservation (REDD+)** credits avoided deforestation. Prices vary widely with baseline credibility and quality ratings.
- **Reforestation and afforestation** remove carbon and usually command more.

Both carry **permanence and reversal** risk from fire or logging, which programmes manage with buffer pools.

### 3. Methane capture

Landfill, agricultural and industrial methane projects deliver large, measurable reductions. Prices depend on additionality, local regulation and methodology.

### 4. Energy efficiency

Improved cookstoves, industrial efficiency and household devices are often valued for health and community co-benefits. Measurement quality affects their price.

### 5. Carbon removal

Biochar, direct air capture and enhanced weathering are costly to develop and monitor but store carbon durably. Prices are typically the highest in the market. Public price data for direct air capture and enhanced weathering is limited, because many deals are private forward purchases.

**Price does not prove quality.** Some low-priced credits are credible, and some expensive ones are not. Check the evidence.

## Voluntary vs Compliance Carbon Credit Price Chart

![Three cards comparing a voluntary credit at $6.92 per tonne, a CORSIA phase 1 unit at $13.15 per tonne and an EU ETS allowance at about €90 per tonne, shown separately because they are different instruments](/media/carbon-price-chart-instruments.png)

*Sources: Sylvera Q3 2026 via public price tracker (reviewed 5 October 2026); [green.earth](https://www.green.earth/news/corsia-activity-builds-across-prices-airlines-and-supply) (16 October 2026); [GMK Center](https://gmk.center/en/news/european-carbon-prices-exceeded-e90-t-in-november/) citing ICE December futures (1 November 2026).*

- **Voluntary transactions** are project- and deal-specific, and price data is fragmented.
- **ETS allowance prices** come from exchange trading in government-regulated systems, so they are easy to observe.
- **Carbon taxes** are set by governments as fixed rates per tonne. They are not traded and are not credit prices.
- **Compliance demand** and restrictions on which units can be used push allowance prices well above voluntary credit prices.

| Comparison factor | Voluntary carbon market | Compliance carbon market |
|---|---|---|
| Main purpose | Voluntary climate action and eligible claims | Meeting regulatory obligations |
| Price formation | Specific to the project and transaction | Depends on the market or government mechanism |
| Tradable units | Carbon credits | Allowances and permitted units |
| Market rules | Programme and buyer requirements | Applicable regulations |
| Price data | Often fragmented | Often easy to observe for major ETSs |

Allowances and voluntary credits are **not interchangeable**. A voluntary credit cannot be surrendered in the EU ETS.

## Carbon Credit Price in India

India's **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS)**, administered by the **Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE)** under the Ministry of Power, creates the Indian Carbon Market. It has two mechanisms:

- a **compliance mechanism**, with emission intensity targets for notified sectors
- an **offset mechanism** for voluntary projects

Both issue **Carbon Credit Certificates (CCCs)**, which will trade on CERC-approved power exchanges.

**As of November 2026, no official CCC trading price had been published.** Exchange trading had not yet started at scale, and a November 2026 report quoted BEE as saying trading would begin for most sectors by the end of 2027. CCCs are domestic instruments, separate from the international voluntary credits that many Indian projects sell. International voluntary prices should **not** be treated as the price of CCCs. Our guide to [carbon credits regulations in India](/blog/carbon-credits-regulations-in-india/) explains the scheme.

## CORSIA Carbon Credit Prices and Eligible Emissions Units

**CORSIA**, the Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation, is ICAO's market-based measure for international aviation's CO₂. Airlines with offsetting requirements cancel **CORSIA eligible emissions units**. These are credits from programmes the ICAO Council approves for each compliance period, which must also meet unit-level conditions:

- scope
- vintage, which for the first phase is 2021–2026
- host-country authorisation and a corresponding adjustment
- a CORSIA label in the registry

Eligibility limits supply, so these units have traded above the average voluntary credit:

| Date | CORSIA first-phase figure | Type | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 28 Jan 2026 | USD 19.00 per tCO₂e | Spot price assessment | [Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/corsia-spot-prices-continue-to-slide-all-eyes-still-on-supply/) |
| Mid-Oct 2026 | USD 13.15 per tCO₂e | Assessment, Dec 2026 delivery | green.earth |
| 18 Nov 2026 | USD 13.00 per tCO₂e | Spot price assessment | [Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/corsia-prices-tick-higher-on-increased-buyer-inquiries-despite-overall-airline-buying-remaining-measured/) |

Prices fell as more labelled units reached the market. Ordinary voluntary credit prices **cannot be treated as CORSIA prices**, and not every eligible unit sells for the same amount. ## Factors That Influence Carbon Credit Prices

![Diagram of the factors that influence a carbon credit's price: supply and demand, project type, quality and integrity, eligibility, vintage, authorisation, standard and method, and buyer preferences](/media/carbon-price-chart-factors.png)

- **Supply and demand**, including deadlines and liquidity
- **Project category**, such as reduction compared with removal
- **Quality and integrity**, including additionality, permanence, reversal risk and leakage
- **Verification and certification**, meaning the programme and auditor
- **Vintage**, meaning the year of the reduction
- **Registry and methodology version**
- **Market regulations and eligibility**, such as CORSIA
- **Buyer preferences**, such as co-benefits or location
- **Host-country authorisation and corresponding adjustments**, where relevant

These factors combine. Two credits of the same type can still differ in price because of vintage, rating or authorisation status.

## How to Read and Analyse a Carbon Credit Price Chart

- **Axes:** the x-axis shows time, and the y-axis shows price per tCO₂e.
- **Frequency:** daily, monthly, quarterly or annual. Annual averages smooth out short-term swings.
- **Averages vs quotes:** a historical average is not today's offer.
- **Indices vs project prices:** an index tracks a basket of credits, while a project price is for one specific credit.
- **Spot vs futures:** check which contract and delivery date a figure refers to.
- **Volatility:** a sharp move may reflect a single event, such as a new supply batch.
- **Currency:** EUR and USD charts are not directly comparable.
- **Source and date:** always note who published the figure and when.

### Illustrative interpretation (hypothetical)

*These numbers are illustrative, not real market data.* Suppose a chart shows an index rising from 8 to 12 in six months. Before concluding that "carbon credits got more expensive", check four things:

- Is it one category or a mix?
- Did the basket change?
- Is it spot or futures?
- Was there a one-off event, such as a regulatory approval?

The same chart can mean very different things depending on these answers.

## Future Outlook of Carbon Credit Prices

Factors that may shape future prices:

- **participation** in carbon markets, voluntary and compliance
- **voluntary demand**, which depends on corporate commitments and scrutiny of claims
- **new compliance markets**, such as India's CCTS
- **CORSIA**, whose second phase starts in 2027 and whose eligible supply depends on ICAO approvals and host-country authorisations
- **availability of high-integrity credits**, and the growing price gap between high- and low-rated credits
- **Article 6** cooperation and authorised international transfers
- **growth in carbon removal**, which may lower costs over time
- **better transparency and price reporting**

These are **possibilities, not forecasts**. The historical data above shows prices can rise and fall sharply.

## Key Takeaways

A carbon credit price chart is only as good as its data. The figures here show:

- a voluntary market that averaged USD 2.5–7.4 per tonne from 2019 to 2024
- a 2026 average around USD 7
- CORSIA units assessed at USD 13–19
- removals priced far higher

Prices depend on project type, integrity, market rules, eligibility, supply, demand and deal terms. Before comparing any two figures, check the source, the reporting date, the unit and the market segment.
