**What is the carbon credit price now?** There is **no single price**. As of **23 November 2026**, the latest dated figures are:

- **about USD 6.92 per tCO₂e** for the average voluntary credit retired in **Q3 2026** (Sylvera)
- **about USD 13.00 per tCO₂e** for CORSIA first-phase units in a spot assessment on **18 November 2026** (Fastmarkets)
- **around €90 per tonne** for EU emissions allowances in **mid-November 2026**, a separate compliance instrument

Prices vary by market, project type, quality and vintage, so **always check the date and source** of any quote. For aviation-specific guidance, see our [CORSIA Carbon Credits](https://corsiacarboncredit.in/) resources.

**Carbon credit pricing** is the amount a buyer pays for one carbon credit, generally representing one metric tonne of CO₂ equivalent (tCO₂e) reduced or removed. Prices are not set by any authority. They emerge from deals between buyers and sellers and are tracked through averages, assessments and indices.

This article is a **dated snapshot of the market now**, with the source, date, unit and type of every figure. It also explains what moves prices and how to use them.

*Type of figure:* average of credits retired, which reflects many different deals, not a live quote.

## Is There One Fixed Price for All Carbon Credits?

**No.** Every credit represents one tonne, but buyers value tonnes differently because of:

- **different standards**, such as Verra, Gold Standard or the American Carbon Registry
- **different project categories**, such as forestry, methane or renewables
- **avoidance versus removal**
- **location and methodology**
- **vintage**, the year the reduction happened
- **environmental integrity** and independent ratings
- **permanence and reversal risks**
- **buyer requirements**, such as CORSIA eligibility
- **supply and demand** for each type
- **market liquidity**, meaning how easily a credit can be bought or sold

**Practical example (illustrative):** Credit A is a 2016 renewable energy credit from a large wind farm in a country where wind power is already cheap. Credit B is a 2024 reforestation credit with a high rating and documented biodiversity benefits. Both represent one tonne, but Credit B might sell for several times Credit A's price, because buyers are more confident its tonne is additional and because removals are scarcer.

## Carbon Credit Price by Market Type

### Voluntary Carbon Market Price

In the **voluntary carbon market**, companies buy credits by choice, usually to meet net-zero or climate targets. They buy through brokers, marketplaces, exchanges or direct deals with developers.

- **Quality strongly affects price**, as the widening rating gap shows.
- **Market assessments** estimate where a credit type would trade. **Transaction prices** are what was actually paid.
- Prices vary widely because credits differ so much and most trades are private.

Our guide to the [carbon credit marketplace](/blog/what-is-a-carbon-credit-marketplace/) explains how these trades work.

### Compliance Carbon Market Price

**Compliance markets** are created by law. In an **emissions trading system**, a government sets a **cap** on emissions and issues **allowances**, permits to emit one tonne each. Covered companies must surrender enough allowances to cover their emissions, and can trade them.

- **Supply** is set by the cap, which falls over time.
- **Demand** depends on industrial output, energy prices and the weather.
- **Regulatory changes** can move prices sharply.

EU allowances traded **around €90 per tonne** in mid-November 2026. Allowances are **not voluntary carbon credits**. They are a different instrument, and their price should not be used to value voluntary credits. The World Bank's Carbon Pricing Dashboard tracks compliance prices and carbon taxes, not voluntary credit prices.

### CORSIA Carbon Credit Price

**CORSIA**, the Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation, is **ICAO's** global market-based measure for international aviation. Airlines with offsetting requirements must cancel **CORSIA eligible emissions units**, creating compliance demand.

Units must come from a **programme approved by the ICAO Council** and meet **unit-level conditions**. For the first phase (2024–2026), these include eligible vintages, host-country authorisation with a corresponding adjustment, and CORSIA labelling in the registry.

Because labelled supply is limited, CORSIA units have been assessed **above** the average voluntary price. Fastmarkets reported that more than **55 million tCO₂e** had been tagged as Phase 1 eligible by November 2026. IATA estimated demand at **200–250 million units** for the first phase.

**There is no guaranteed universal CORSIA price**, and ICAO does not set one. Our guide to the [CORSIA carbon credit price in 2026](/blog/corsia-carbon-credit-price-2026/) covers this market in more detail.

### Carbon Credit Price in India

- **Voluntary market:** Indian projects sell credits internationally under programmes such as Verra and Gold Standard, at **international voluntary prices**.
- **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS):** administered by the **Bureau of Energy Efficiency** under the **Ministry of Power**. It issues **Carbon Credit Certificates (CCCs)**, through a compliance mechanism for obligated industries and an offset mechanism for voluntary projects.
- **Trading:** CCCs will trade on power exchanges under **Central Electricity Regulatory Commission** regulations notified in February 2026, with **Grid Controller of India** as the registry.

CCCs and voluntary credits are **different instruments**. **No official CCC price was available as of November 2026**, and voluntary credit prices should not be presented as CCC prices.

## Carbon Credit Price by Project Type

### Renewable Energy Carbon Credits

Solar, wind and hydro credits are abundant, and many come from older projects whose additionality is questioned. The latest category average we found is **USD 2.67 per tCO₂e (2024, Ecosystem Marketplace)**. Many programmes now restrict new grid-connected renewable projects. Our guide on [calculating carbon credits from solar](https://corsiacarboncredit.in/blog/how-do-you-calculate-carbon-credits-from-solar/) explains these rules.

### Forestry and REDD+ Carbon Credits

These protect existing forests from deforestation. Prices depend heavily on baseline credibility. In **Q3 2026**, Sylvera reported **USD 3.56 (lower-rated) to USD 7.75 (higher-rated)** per tCO₂e.

### Afforestation and Reforestation Credits

These remove CO₂ by planting or restoring forests, with monitoring and buffers for reversal risk. In **Q3 2026**, they averaged **USD 12.80 to 23.47** per tCO₂e, depending on rating (Sylvera).

### Methane Capture Carbon Credits

Landfill, livestock and wastewater methane projects deliver large, measurable reductions. **We found no reliable current public category average**, because prices vary with methodology, location and local regulation.

### Waste Management Carbon Credits

Landfill gas recovery, composting and organic waste treatment projects overlap with methane credits. **No reliable current public average** was available.

### Agricultural and Soil Carbon Credits

Soil carbon is harder to measure and can be reversed if practices stop. This is a small, developing segment, and **we found no reliable public 2026 average**.

### Biochar Carbon Credits

Biochar stores carbon in a stable form for long periods, with strict methodology and durability requirements. Ecosystem Marketplace reported **over USD 160 per tCO₂e in 2024**.

### Direct Air Capture Carbon Credits

Direct air capture (DAC) offers high-durability geological storage but is expensive to build and run. **Reliable public prices are not available**, because most DAC credits are sold through private, multi-year forward contracts.

## What Factors Determine the Price of One Carbon Credit?

1. **Project category:** removals and newer technologies cost more than abundant avoidance credits.
2. **Project quality and integrity:** high ratings attract more buyers.
3. **Additionality:** buyers pay more when the project clearly needed carbon finance.
4. **Removal versus avoidance:** removals are scarcer and preferred for net-zero claims.
5. **Certification programme:** reputation and rules matter.
6. **Approved methodology:** some methodologies, such as those with the ICVCM's Core Carbon Principles label, carry more buyer trust.
7. **Vintage:** newer credits are usually preferred.
8. **Permanence and reversal risk:** longer, safer storage commands more.
9. **Leakage:** higher risk lowers confidence.
10. **Independent verification:** quality audits support value.
11. **Co-benefits:** biodiversity and community benefits can add value.
12. **Registry and transaction costs:** these add to the final price.
13. **Supply and demand:** scarcity of a credit type raises its price.
14. **Corporate climate commitments:** these drive voluntary demand.
15. **Government policies:** rules such as CORSIA and Article 6 create new demand.
16. **Buyer-specific requirements:** eligibility needs narrow the pool of suitable credits.

The more of these a credit meets, the more buyers are usually willing to pay. But **a higher price does not automatically mean a better credit**.

## How Is the Price of One Carbon Credit Calculated?

> **Total purchase cost = Number of carbon credits × Price per credit**

*All examples are hypothetical, not current quotes or guaranteed prices.*

- **Example 1: Small business purchase.** 100 credits × USD 10 = **USD 1,000**
- **Example 2: Medium-sized corporate purchase.** 1,000 credits × USD 15 = **USD 15,000**
- **Example 3: Large corporate purchase.** 10,000 credits × USD 20 = **USD 200,000**

The final cost may also include:

- **brokerage fees**
- **registry transfer and retirement charges**
- **marketplace fees**
- **due diligence costs**, such as ratings or legal review
- **contractual and administrative costs**

## Why Do Carbon Credit Prices Increase or Decrease?

- **Demand changes:** more corporate buying raises prices, while scaled-back pledges lower them.
- **Supply of verified credits:** CORSIA prices fell during 2026 as labelled supply grew.
- **Availability of high-quality removals:** limited supply supports removal prices.
- **Corporate net-zero commitments:** these set the long-term demand base.
- **International aviation demand:** CORSIA compliance deadlines create buying pressure.
- **New regulations:** Article 6 rules, national schemes and claim rules shape demand.
- **Integrity initiatives:** ICVCM labels and ratings increase demand for top-tier credits and reduce it for weaker ones.
- **Methodology changes:** stricter rules can cut supply or devalue older credits.
- **Economic conditions:** tighter budgets reduce discretionary buying.
- **Buyer confidence:** media scrutiny has repeatedly hit demand for some credit types.

The **widening quality gap** in 2026 shows several of these forces at once: demand moved towards higher-rated credits while lower-rated ones stayed cheap.

## How Can Buyers Find the Current Carbon Credit Price?

- **Carbon credit marketplaces:** listed asking prices.
- **Market data providers:** for example, Sylvera, Ecosystem Marketplace and AlliedOffsets.
- **Price reporting agencies:** for example, Fastmarkets and S&P Global Platts, which publish assessments.
- **Exchanges and indices:** for example, ICE lists CORSIA eligible emissions unit futures.
- **Broker quotations.**
- **Direct project developer quotations.**
- **Registry information:** to confirm a credit exists and is unretired.

To compare fairly, use quotes for the **same**:

- project category
- vintage
- certification programme
- market segment
- transaction size
- delivery and retirement conditions

**Registries record a credit's status and ownership, not its live market price.**

## How Can Sellers Get the Best Price for Carbon Credits?

- **Choose an appropriate programme** that your target buyers accept.
- **Keep high-quality documentation**, including design and monitoring reports.
- **Demonstrate additionality** clearly.
- **Ensure reliable monitoring and verification.**
- **Understand buyer eligibility requirements**, such as CORSIA labels or ratings.
- **Compare several buyer quotations.**
- **Choose between spot and forward contracts**, balancing price certainty and timing.
- **Evaluate intermediary fees.**
- **Maintain transparent registry records.**

Quality and certification can **improve** buyer interest, but they **do not guarantee a premium price**. Our guide on [how much you can earn from carbon credits](/blog/how-much-can-you-earn-from-carbon-credits/) covers revenue and costs for sellers.

## Are Carbon Credit Prices Expected to Rise in the Future?

**Current data:** voluntary averages rose year on year in Q3 2026, while CORSIA units fell from USD 19.00 to about USD 13.00 during 2026.

**Historical trends:** Ecosystem Marketplace's annual voluntary averages rose from about **USD 2.51 (2020)** to **USD 7.37 (2022)**, then eased to about **USD 6.34 (2024)**.

**Forecasts:** Sylvera has modelled CORSIA Phase 1 prices in a range of **USD 15–53 per unit by January 2028**. That is a **single forecaster's scenario range**, not a consensus.

**Factors that could push prices up or down:**

- growth or retreat in corporate climate commitments
- aviation demand as CORSIA deadlines approach, and Phase 2 from 2027
- demand for high-durability removals
- new supply of verified and authorised credits
- Article 6 and national policies
- better carbon accounting and verification

**This article does not predict or guarantee future prices or returns.**

## Common Mistakes When Researching Carbon Credit Prices

- **Assuming all credits cost the same**
- **Comparing voluntary credits with compliance allowances**
- **Using outdated quotes**, such as a January CORSIA price in November
- **Confusing asking prices with completed transactions**
- **Ignoring vintage**
- **Ignoring certification and methodology**
- **Assuming all CORSIA-related credits are interchangeable**, since Phase 1 and Phase 2 units differ
- **Treating a marketplace listing as a completed sale**
- **Ignoring transaction and registry fees**
- **Trusting unsupported price forecasts**
