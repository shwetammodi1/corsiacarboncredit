**What is CORSIA carbon credits insurance?** It is insurance that protects carbon market participants against **specified risks** in CORSIA-related carbon credit projects and transactions. The best-known product **covers the risk that a host country fails to apply a promised corresponding adjustment**. If that happens, the policy provides a replacement unit, or money to buy one.

Under CORSIA's first-phase rules, programmes must deal with this risk, and some programmes, including **Gold Standard** and the **Climate Action Reserve**, accept **approved insurance** as one way to do so. However:

- **Insurance is not a general ICAO requirement** for every CORSIA unit.
- **Insurance cannot make an ineligible credit eligible.**

For more background, see our [CORSIA Carbon Credits](https://corsiacarboncredit.in/) resources.

## Introduction to CORSIA Carbon Credits Insurance

**CORSIA**, the Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation, is ICAO's global market-based measure for international aviation. Airlines with offsetting requirements must **cancel eligible emissions units**. That makes carbon credits a **compliance tool** for aviation, not just a voluntary choice.

Compliance raises the stakes. If an airline cancels a unit that later proves ineligible or double-counted, it may not meet its obligation. Buyers, sellers and project developers therefore need **risk management**.

**Carbon credit insurance** is one part of that toolkit. It transfers specified financial risks to an insurer in exchange for a premium.

**Insurance is a risk-management tool, not a CORSIA eligibility requirement in itself**, unless a specific programme rule, contract or regulation requires it for a particular unit or transaction. The most important example is the corresponding adjustment rule explained below.

## What Is Carbon Credit Insurance?

**Carbon credit insurance** is a policy that pays out, or provides replacement credits, when a **defined event** affects carbon credits or a carbon project, subject to its terms, limits and exclusions.

It helps participants because carbon projects and transactions carry:

- **financial risks**, such as paying in advance for credits that are never delivered
- **operational risks**, such as project underperformance or fire
- **regulatory and political risks**, such as a government not honouring an authorisation

Insurance is often confused with other protections:

- **Insurance:** a contract with a third-party insurer that pays for covered losses.
- **Contractual warranties:** promises by a seller, such as "these credits are eligible", which give a claim against the seller if untrue.
- **Guarantees:** a third party, such as a bank or parent company, promises to pay if the seller does not.
- **Buffer mechanisms:** credits set aside by a programme to cover reversals across many projects.

Insurance can improve confidence by putting a financially strong insurer behind the risk, especially where the seller alone could not cover a large loss.

## Why Do CORSIA Carbon Credit Transactions Need Risk Management?

- **Eligibility uncertainty:** credits must meet programme, vintage and unit-level rules for the specific compliance period.
- **Verification risks:** verification may find fewer reductions than expected.
- **Delivery delays:** forward deals depend on future issuance.
- **Registry and ownership issues:** the seller must actually hold the units, and labelling must be correct.
- **Regulatory changes:** programme or ICAO rules can be updated.
- **Host-country authorisation:** a Letter of Authorisation (LoA) is needed for first-phase units.
- **Corresponding adjustment risk:** the host country might not apply the adjustment it promised.
- **Project underperformance:** fewer credits than contracted.
- **Invalidation or reversal:** credits may be cancelled for errors or fraud, or stored carbon released.

**Due diligence is essential** before buying for CORSIA compliance, because insurance only covers what the policy says.

## Types of Insurance Relevant to Carbon Credit Projects

*Availability and terms vary by insurer, jurisdiction and transaction. Not every category below is a standard or widely available CORSIA-specific product.*

### 1. Carbon Credit Invalidation Insurance

This covers **specified circumstances** in which credits become invalid or unusable. For CORSIA, the most established version is **corresponding adjustment insurance**, which pays out if a host country does not apply the adjustment it authorised.

Some insurers also offer cover for invalidation caused by **fraud, negligence or errors** in project data, depending on policy wording.

### 2. Carbon Project Performance Insurance

This may cover **specified delivery risks**, such as a project producing fewer credits than contracted because of a defined event. It is often linked to offtake agreements and project finance.

### 3. Environmental Liability Insurance

This is traditional cover that may protect a project against **third-party claims** for environmental damage, such as pollution from a waste facility. It protects the project, not the credits themselves.

### 4. Professional Indemnity Insurance

This covers **consultants, validators, verifiers** and other service providers against claims of professional negligence, such as an error in a validation report.

### 5. Business Interruption Insurance

This may cover **lost income** when a covered event, such as fire or flood, halts project operations, subject to the policy.

### 6. Political Risk Insurance

This covers losses caused by **government action**, such as expropriation, breach of contract or failure to honour commitments. It is directly relevant to CORSIA, because host-country authorisation is a government act. Providers include the World Bank Group's **Multilateral Investment Guarantee Agency (MIGA)** and private political risk insurers.

### 7. Transaction and Contractual Risk Protection

Insurance usually works alongside:

- **warranties and indemnities** in sales contracts
- **escrow**, where payment is held until delivery
- **delivery guarantees** and **replacement obligations**, where the seller must replace undelivered or invalid units

## How Does Insurance Relate to CORSIA's Corresponding Adjustment Rules?

For CORSIA's **first phase (2024–2026)**, units must be backed by **host-country authorisation**, with the host country committing to a **corresponding adjustment** under Article 6 of the Paris Agreement. Programmes must have measures in place in case the adjustment is not applied.

According to industry guidance:

- **Insurance mechanisms** can provide **replacement eligible units**, or funds to buy them, if the host country does not apply the adjustment.
- If a programme finds that the adjustment was not applied, it can issue a **demand notice**, and the developer must replace or compensate for the double-counted unit, supported by the policy.
- Insurance requirements **may not apply** where the host country has **already confirmed the adjustment** in its official Paris Agreement reporting.
- Once units are **labelled CORSIA eligible** in a registry, that label generally **is not changed**, even if an adjustment later fails. That is why compensation arrangements matter.

**Approved insurance providers (as published by programmes):**

- **Gold Standard** has approved CORSIA insurance policies from **MIGA, Artio MGA Services, CFC Underwriting and Oka**. In **July 2026**, it added **Blenheim**, its first traditional political risk insurer. Gold Standard appointed broker **Howden** to assess products against its criteria.
- The **Climate Action Reserve** published **Criteria for Approved Insurance Mechanisms under CORSIA** in August 2026. They require, for example, that a policy specifies a compensation trigger date and can respond when a host country does not apply the required adjustment.

**Each programme sets its own rules.** Check the programme's current requirements and approved lists before relying on a policy.

## What Risks Can Carbon Credit Insurance Cover?

Depending on the policy, potential covered risks include:

- **Corresponding adjustment failure** by a host country
- **Specified project failure**, such as a defined catastrophic event
- **Certain delivery failures** under forward contracts
- **Defined invalidation events**, such as cancellation for specified errors or fraud
- **Environmental liability claims** against a project
- **Professional negligence** by service providers
- **Covered political risks**, such as breach of contract or expropriation
- **Certain business interruption events**

**Coverage always depends on the actual policy wording**, including:

- **exclusions**
- **limits**, the maximum the policy will pay
- **deductibles**, the part of the loss the insured bears
- **insured events**, exactly what triggers a claim

**Example (hypothetical):** a trader sells 100,000 first-phase CORSIA units from a project in Country X, which issued an LoA. The developer bought corresponding adjustment insurance. Country X later fails to apply the adjustment in its reporting. The programme issues a demand notice, and the insurer funds 100,000 replacement units, up to the policy limit. Without insurance, the developer would have to find and pay for replacements itself.

## What Risks May Not Be Covered by Carbon Credit Insurance?

Risks that policies **often exclude**, depending on terms, include:

- **General market price fluctuations**, such as CORSIA prices falling after purchase
- **Normal investment losses**
- **Unspecified regulatory changes**
- **Known or undisclosed project defects**
- **Fraud or intentional misconduct** by the insured, where excluded
- **Credits that were never eligible** under the relevant scheme
- **Failure to meet CORSIA requirements** that the policy does not cover
- **Unauthorised or improperly documented transactions**

These are not universal exclusions. **Insurers decide coverage through individual policy terms**, so read the policy carefully.

## How Does Carbon Credit Insurance Work?

1. **Identify the project or transaction** to be insured.
2. **Assess project and credit risks**, such as authorisation, delivery and reversal.
3. **Carry out technical and legal due diligence.**
4. **Identify insurable risks.**
5. **Approach an appropriate insurer**, often through a specialist broker.
6. **Review policy conditions and exclusions.**
7. **Agree coverage limits and premiums.**
8. **Finalise the policy documents.**
9. **Monitor the project or transaction**, and report material changes to the insurer.
10. **Follow the insurer's claims procedure** if a covered event occurs.

Insurance can apply at different levels:

- **A carbon project:** covering the project's operations or delivery.
- **A credit portfolio:** covering a holder's credits from several projects.
- **A specific transaction:** covering one sale or offtake agreement.

## Is Insurance Mandatory for CORSIA Carbon Credits?

**No, not as a general ICAO condition.** ICAO sets eligibility through:

- **Approved emissions unit programmes**, chosen by the ICAO Council
- **Unit-level conditions** for each compliance period, such as vintage and scope
- **Host-country authorisation and corresponding adjustments** for first-phase units

The ICAO rules require programmes to address the risk that a corresponding adjustment is not applied. **Some programmes accept approved insurance** as one way to meet that requirement for units whose adjustment is not yet confirmed. In those cases, insurance can be **required in practice** for a unit to be labelled.

Two points are clear:

- **Commercial insurance and CORSIA eligibility are different things.** A delivery or performance policy does not affect eligibility.
- **Insurance cannot make an otherwise ineligible credit CORSIA eligible.** A credit with the wrong vintage or an unapproved methodology stays ineligible, insured or not.

Always check ICAO's latest **CORSIA Eligible Emissions Units** document and the programme's current rules. Our guide to [CORSIA eligible carbon credits](/blog/corsia-eligible-carbon-credits/) explains the eligibility checks.

## Carbon Credit Insurance for Buyers and Sellers

### For buyers

- **Eligibility due diligence:** confirm the programme, vintage, label and LoA.
- **Delivery risk assessment:** especially for forward deals.
- **Registry ownership checks:** confirm the seller holds the units.
- **Contractual protection:** warranties, replacement obligations and escrow.
- **Insurance options:** ask whether units carry approved corresponding adjustment cover, and who the insured party is.

### For sellers

- **Delivery risk:** whether the project can produce the contracted volume.
- **Liability exposure:** environmental and third-party claims.
- **Contractual obligations:** replacement duties if units fail.
- **Professional service protection:** for consultants and verifiers working on the project.
- **Risk allocation:** agreeing with buyers who bears which risk, and who pays for insurance.

**Both parties should review insurance coverage alongside the sales contract**, because gaps between the two leave risk uncovered. ## How Much Does Carbon Credit Insurance Cost?

**There is no standard premium rate.** We have not found reliable public premium figures to quote. Premiums depend on:

- **project size** and **credit volume**
- **project location**, including host-country political risk
- **project methodology**
- **project maturity** and **historical performance**
- **the insured risks**
- **coverage limits** and **deductibles**
- **policy duration**
- **the insurer's risk assessment**

For example, cover for a country with a clear, tested authorisation process is likely to be priced differently from cover for a country with no track record. Buyers should factor premiums into the total cost of CORSIA units.

## How to Choose the Right Carbon Credit Insurance Provider

Check:

- **Credentials:** the insurer should be licensed and regulated in the relevant jurisdiction. In India, insurance is regulated by the **Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI)**.
- **Underwriting experience** in carbon or political risk.
- **Policy coverage** and **exclusions**.
- **Claims process:** how claims are made and paid.
- **Financial strength:** ratings and capacity to pay large claims.
- **Jurisdiction and governing law.**
- **Carbon market expertise.**
- **Contractual compatibility** with your sales agreement.
- **Programme approval:** whether the policy appears on the relevant programme's approved list, if CORSIA labelling depends on it.

Different market roles:

- **Insurer:** takes on risk and pays covered claims.
- **Carbon credit supplier:** sells the credits.
- **Broker:** arranges insurance or credit deals for clients.
- **Registry:** records credits and their status.

## Carbon Credit Insurance vs Carbon Credit Buffer Mechanisms

**A buffer pool** is a reserve of credits that a programme holds back from projects. For example, forestry projects contribute a share of their credits based on their reversal risk. If carbon is lost through fire or logging, the programme cancels buffer credits to compensate.

| | Buffer pool | Insurance |
|---|---|---|
| **Who provides it** | The crediting programme, using credits from many projects | A third-party insurer |
| **What it covers** | Mainly reversals of stored carbon | Risks defined in the policy, such as corresponding adjustment failure or delivery |
| **How it pays** | Cancels buffer credits | Pays money or provides replacement credits |
| **Who it protects** | The integrity of the programme's credits overall | The insured party |

**A buffer contribution is not an insurance policy.** Programme rules decide which safeguards apply. Some projects have both. Our guide to [how carbon credits are created](/blog/how-are-carbon-credits-created/) explains where buffer contributions fit in the issuance process.

## Future of Carbon Credit Insurance in the CORSIA Market

**Current developments:**

- Programmes such as Gold Standard and the Climate Action Reserve have published **insurance criteria or approved lists** for CORSIA.
- **Political risk insurers** and **specialist carbon insurers** have entered the market.
- Supply of authorised units remains short of demand. IATA estimated first-phase demand at 200–250 million units against about 72 million authorised in August 2026.

**Possible developments, not predictions:**

- clearer rules for the **second phase (2027–2035)**
- more **transparent underwriting** and standard policy wording
- wider use of insurance in **offtake and project finance**
- more host countries confirming adjustments, which could **reduce** the need for corresponding adjustment insurance

Research groups have also published discussion papers on whether insurance can address **reversal risk** more broadly, which remains an open question.

## Conclusion

Insurance has become an important part of the CORSIA market, mainly because of **corresponding adjustment risk**. Programmes such as Gold Standard and the Climate Action Reserve accept **approved insurance** as a way to compensate if a host country does not deliver the adjustment it promised. Other policies, such as political risk, performance and liability cover, help manage project and transaction risks.

But insurance is **not a general ICAO requirement**, **cannot make an ineligible credit eligible**, and usually **does not cover price risk**. It works best alongside thorough due diligence, clear contracts and correct registry records.
