## Introduction to Carbon Credit Prices in the UK

A **carbon credit** represents **one metric tonne of CO₂ equivalent (tCO₂e)** reduced or removed by a project, such as planting woodland or restoring peatland.

Carbon prices have monetary value because:

- **companies covered by the UK ETS** must hold allowances for their emissions
- **companies with net-zero targets** buy credits voluntarily
- **airlines** must meet CORSIA obligations on some international routes

The UK carbon market has two main parts:

1. **Compliance:** the **UK Emissions Trading Scheme (UK ETS)**, where covered companies trade **UK Allowances (UKAs)**.
2. **Voluntary:** UK **Woodland Carbon Code** and **Peatland Code** units, as well as international voluntary credits bought by UK companies.

**These instruments have different prices** because they serve different purposes and buyers.

## Understanding the UK Emissions Trading Scheme (UK ETS)

### What it is and why it was introduced

The **UK ETS** began on **1 January 2021**, replacing the UK's participation in the EU Emissions Trading System after Brexit. It is run by the **UK ETS Authority**: the UK Government, Scottish Government, Welsh Government and Northern Ireland's Department of Agriculture, Environment and Rural Affairs.

### How cap-and-trade works

1. The Authority sets a **cap** on total emissions from covered sectors, which falls over time.
2. It creates **UK Allowances (UKAs)** up to the cap, each allowing one tonne of CO₂e.
3. Some UKAs are **given free** to industries at risk of moving production abroad. The rest are **auctioned**.
4. Covered companies must **surrender** enough UKAs each year to cover their emissions.
5. Companies that cut emissions can **sell** spare allowances. Those that need more must **buy** them.

### Auctions and secondary trading

- **Auctions** are held regularly on ICE Futures Europe. The first auction, on **19 May 2021**, cleared at **£43.99**.
- An **auction reserve price** of **£28** sets a minimum for auctions.
- After auction, UKAs trade on the **secondary market**, mainly as futures.

### Who is covered

The UK ETS covers:

- **power generation**
- **energy-intensive industries**
- **aviation**, including domestic UK flights and flights from the UK to the European Economic Area

The government has announced plans to expand it to **domestic maritime** and **waste**.

### UKAs vs project-based offset credits

- A **UKA** is a **permit to emit**, created by the government under the cap.
- An **offset credit** represents a **reduction or removal** achieved by a project.

UK ETS participants **cannot use** Woodland Carbon Code or other offset credits instead of UKAs for compliance.

## Voluntary Carbon Credit Prices in the UK

### What voluntary credits are

**Voluntary carbon credits** are bought by choice, usually by companies for net-zero or climate claims. UK companies buy either **UK-based units**, from the Woodland Carbon Code or Peatland Code, or **international credits** from programmes such as Verra and Gold Standard.

### How UK companies buy them

- directly from **project developers** or landowners
- through **brokers and marketplaces**
- via the **UK Land Carbon Registry**, which records UK woodland and peatland units

### How project quality affects prices

Buyers pay more for **verified** units, **domestic** projects, strong **permanence** and **co-benefits** such as biodiversity and flood protection.

### The Woodland Carbon Code

The **Woodland Carbon Code (WCC)** is the UK standard for woodland creation projects that remove CO₂. It issues:

- **Pending Issuance Units (PIUs):** a **promise** of future carbon removal, based on the predicted growth of trees. A PIU becomes a WCU only after verification.
- **Woodland Carbon Units (WCUs):** **verified** units, issued after an independent check confirms the carbon has actually been removed.

### The Peatland Code

The **Peatland Code**, run by the IUCN UK Peatland Programme, covers **peatland restoration**, which reduces emissions from damaged peat. It issues:

- **PIUs:** predicted future emission reductions.
- **Peatland Carbon Units (PCUs):** verified reductions.

### Why a PIU is not the same as a verified unit

**A PIU is not a verified carbon credit.** It is a promise that may or may not be fully delivered. Under the codes' rules, companies **should not use PIUs to claim they have offset current emissions**. They can only report them as future planned reductions. This is one reason PIUs and international verified credits should not be compared directly.

## Factors Affecting Carbon Credit Prices in the UK

- **Supply and demand:** for UKAs, the cap and auction volumes against industrial demand. For voluntary units, project supply against corporate demand.
- **UK government climate policies:** carbon budgets and net-zero targets.
- **The UK ETS cap and allowance supply:** a tighter cap supports higher prices.
- **Industrial emissions:** higher output increases demand for UKAs.
- **Corporate net-zero commitments:** drive voluntary demand.
- **Project quality:** verified, well-documented units are worth more.
- **Methodology:** each code sets its own rules for calculating removals.
- **Permanence and additionality:** buyers value long-term storage and projects that need carbon finance.
- **Credit vintage:** the timing of delivery, which matters especially for PIUs.
- **Market confidence:** policy uncertainty can cause sharp moves.
- **Regulatory changes:** such as UK–EU linking or ETS expansion.
- **International developments:** EU carbon prices strongly influence UKAs.

**Key differences:**

- **Regulatory frameworks:** the UK and EU ETS are government cap-and-trade schemes. ACCUs and Indian CCCs are government-issued credits. Voluntary credits come from independent programmes. CORSIA units follow ICAO rules.
- **Market instruments:** allowances are permits to emit, while credits represent reductions or removals.
- **Eligibility:** each scheme accepts only its own instruments.
- **Currency:** £, €, A$, ₹ and US$.
- **Trading:** UKAs and EUAs trade on exchanges. Voluntary credits trade mostly over the counter.
- **Price formation:** compliance demand generally supports higher prices.

Allowances and project credits are **not interchangeable**. ## How Can Businesses Buy Carbon Credits in the UK?

1. **Identify the requirement:** compliance under the UK ETS, or a voluntary climate target.
2. **Understand compliance versus voluntary demand:**
   - **UK ETS participants** need **UKAs**, obtained through auctions, free allocation or the secondary market, usually via a broker or exchange member.
   - **Voluntary buyers** choose credits that support their claims.
3. **Select appropriate credits:** UK woodland or peatland units, or international verified credits.
4. **Check project documentation:** design, validation and verification reports.
5. **Verify registry information:** for example, on the **UK Land Carbon Registry**.
6. **Assess quality:** whether units are verified or PIUs, and their permanence and co-benefits.
7. **Understand transaction costs:** brokerage, registry and retirement fees.
8. **Complete the purchase and retirement or surrender:**
   - **UKAs** are **surrendered** in the UK ETS registry for compliance.
   - **Voluntary credits** are **retired** in the relevant registry, in the buyer's name.

## Future Outlook for UK Carbon Credit Prices

**Current developments:**

- **UK–EU linking:** the UK and EU agreed in May 2025 to work towards linking their emissions trading systems, and aimed to conclude negotiations at the **UK–EU summit on 20 November 2026**. Reports around the summit indicated a target to apply the link from **1 January 2028**, subject to ratification. Check the official UK Government and European Commission statements for the final terms.
- **ETS expansion** to new sectors, such as maritime and waste.
- **UK climate policy:** legally binding carbon budgets and net zero by 2050.

**Possible influences, not forecasts:**

- **price convergence** between UKAs and EU allowances if linking goes ahead
- **corporate decarbonisation demand** for voluntary units
- **integrity scrutiny** of voluntary markets, which could favour verified units
- growth in **nature-based projects** such as woodland and peatland
- **future supply** of verified WCUs and PCUs

Prices could rise or fall. **No price increase or return is guaranteed.**
