As buyers shift from avoidance credits toward genuine **removals**, one option keeps rising up the shortlist: **biochar**. It sits in a sweet spot — more durable than a forest, far cheaper than direct air capture, and available at scale today. Here is how biochar carbon credits work and why they are in demand.

## What Biochar Actually Is

Biochar is a stable, charcoal-like solid made by heating biomass — crop residues, forestry waste, other organic matter — in a low-oxygen process called **pyrolysis**. Left to decompose, that biomass would release its carbon back to the atmosphere within a few years. Converted to biochar, the carbon is locked into a form that resists breakdown for a very long time. A biochar credit represents that avoided return of carbon to the air, stored durably.

## Why Durability Is the Headline

The defining question for any removal is **permanence** — how long the carbon stays out of the atmosphere. This is where biochar shines:

| Removal type | Typical durability | Relative cost |
|---|---|---|
| Forestry (avoidance/ARR) | Decades, reversible | Low–mid |
| Biochar | Centuries | Mid |
| Direct air capture + storage | Millennia | High |

A forest can burn; biochar, once in the ground or in a durable use, does not simply reverse. That century-scale durability is why biochar is treated as a serious removal rather than a temporary store — and why it commands a premium over avoidance credits.

## The Co-Benefits Buyers Like

Biochar is unusual in that the removal often comes with a productive use. Applied to soil, it can improve water retention, reduce the need for fertiliser, and lift yields — so the same tonne of stored carbon also delivers agricultural benefit. These **co-benefits** strengthen the development story behind the credit and make biochar attractive to buyers who want more than a number.

## Integrity and MRV

Biochar's integrity case is comparatively strong because the removal is **measurable**. Unlike below-ground forest carbon, the quantity of carbon in a batch of biochar can be characterised in the lab, and the process is a discrete, verifiable industrial step. Methodologies and registries for biochar have matured quickly, and third-party verification follows the same logic as any credit. The main diligence questions are the feedstock's sustainability, the durability of the end use, and — as always — conservative quantification. For the wider quality framework, see [how to evaluate carbon offset quality](/insights/evaluating-carbon-offset-quality-airlines/).

## Where Biochar Sits in a Portfolio

For a buyer building a credible removal portfolio, biochar is often the workhorse: durable enough to count as a real removal, measurable enough to defend, and affordable enough to buy at volume — a practical middle ground between cheap-but-reversible forestry and expensive-but-permanent [direct air capture](/insights/direct-air-capture-carbon-removal/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are biochar carbon credits?** Credits representing carbon durably stored as biochar — a stable solid made by heating biomass — which would otherwise have decomposed and released its carbon.

**How long does biochar store carbon?** Typically centuries, making it far more durable than forestry and one of the more permanent removals available at scale today.

**Are biochar credits removals or avoidance?** Removals — they represent carbon taken out of the natural cycle and stored durably, not an avoided future emission.

**What are the co-benefits of biochar?** Applied to soil it can improve water retention, reduce fertiliser needs and increase yields, adding agricultural value to the carbon removal.

**Why are biochar credits considered high integrity?** The stored carbon is measurable, the process is a discrete verifiable step, and durability is high — though feedstock sustainability and conservative quantification still matter.
