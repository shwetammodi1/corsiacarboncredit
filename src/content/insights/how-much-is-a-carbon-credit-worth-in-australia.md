## Introduction – Understanding the Value of a Carbon Credit in Australia

A **carbon credit** represents one tonne of greenhouse gas emissions avoided or stored by an eligible project. In Australia, the main carbon credit is the **ACCU**, issued by the federal **Clean Energy Regulator (CER)**. One ACCU generally represents **1 tCO₂-e**, under the scheme's rules.

Carbon credits have monetary value because **someone needs or wants them**:

- **Large industrial facilities** covered by the **Safeguard Mechanism** use ACCUs to help meet declining emissions limits.
- **The Australian Government** has bought ACCUs through contracts.
- **Companies** buy them for voluntary climate commitments.

Prices matter to:

- **businesses**, because they set the cost of compliance and climate claims
- **project developers**, because they decide whether a project is financially viable
- **landholders and investors**, because they shape potential income

## What Is an Australian Carbon Credit Unit (ACCU)?

### Definition

An ACCU is a financial product issued by the Clean Energy Regulator, representing **one tonne of CO₂-e** stored or avoided by a registered project. ACCUs are created under the **Carbon Credits (Carbon Farming Initiative) Act 2011**, through what is now called the **ACCU Scheme**.

### How ACCUs are generated

1. A project registers with the CER under an approved **method**, the rulebook for that project type.
2. It must meet **additionality** requirements, including being new and not required by law.
3. It carries out the activity and monitors results.
4. It submits reports, with **audits** by registered auditors at set points.
5. The CER **issues** ACCUs for the verified abatement.

### The role of the Clean Energy Regulator

The CER registers projects, issues ACCUs, runs the **Australian National Registry of Emissions Units (ANREU)**, administers the Safeguard Mechanism, and publishes the **Quarterly Carbon Market Report**.

### How ACCUs are issued and traded

ACCUs are issued into the project owner's ANREU account. They are traded **mainly over the counter** through brokers, sold directly to buyers, or delivered to the government under contracts.

### How businesses use or surrender ACCUs

- **Safeguard facilities** surrender ACCUs to offset emissions above their baselines.
- **Companies** can cancel ACCUs voluntarily to support claims, such as carbon neutral certification.
- Once surrendered or cancelled, ACCUs **cannot be used again**.

## Factors That Determine the Value of Australian Carbon Credits

- **Supply and demand:** the CER expected **22 to 26 million ACCUs** to be issued in 2026, against growing Safeguard demand.
- **Government climate policies:** declining Safeguard baselines create compliance demand.
- **ACCU Scheme regulations:** new or revised methods change supply.
- **Project methodology:** buyers may value some methods more than others.
- **Project quality and integrity:** concerns about a method can lower demand for its ACCUs.
- **Corporate demand:** voluntary buyers add to compliance demand.
- **Market confidence:** policy surprises can move prices sharply.
- **Credit availability:** issuance timing affects how many ACCUs are ready to sell.
- **Regulatory changes:** reviews, such as the Climate Change Authority's 2026 ACCU Scheme review, can shift expectations.
- **Delivery and reversal risks:** forward deals carry delivery risk, and vegetation projects carry reversal risk, for example from fire.

**Different instruments have different values** because they serve different purposes and buyers. An ACCU, a voluntary credit and a European allowance each have their own market.

## Australian Carbon Credit Price History

*All figures are generic ACCU spot prices in A$ per ACCU, nominal, from the Clean Energy Regulator unless stated.*

- **2015–2020:** the government bought ACCUs through Emissions Reduction Fund auctions. The first three averaged about **A$11.83**, and fixed delivery contracts averaged **A$16.32** (March 2020) and **A$15.53** (September 2020).
- **January 2021:** about **A$16.50** (RepuTex).
- **Start of 2022:** about **A$51**, after a record year of corporate and investor demand.
- **Late January 2022:** all-time high of **A$57.50**, on low volumes and speculative trading.
- **March 2022:** the government announced that projects could **exit fixed delivery contracts** and sell on the open market. The price fell to **A$30.50** by 31 March.
- **2022–2023:** integrity concerns led to the **Chubb Review**. The government's response, in January 2023, found the scheme essentially sound. The reformed **Safeguard Mechanism** began on 1 July 2023. Prices were **A$33.80** at the end of 2022 and **A$33.75** at the end of 2023.
- **2024:** prices reached **A$42.50** on 19 November 2024 before ending the year at **A$36.63**.
- **2025:** **A$36.60** at year end.
- **2026:** **A$36.28** at the end of March, **A$37.95** at the end of June, **A$39.03** on 14 August, and **A$37.95** at the 20 November close (CORE Markets).

## Are All Carbon Credits Worth the Same Amount in Australia?

**No.** Different instruments trade at different prices:

- **ACCUs:** government-issued Australian credits, usable for Safeguard compliance. About **A$37.95** on 20 November 2026.
- **Voluntary carbon credits:** issued by independent programmes such as Verra and Gold Standard. The average credit retired worldwide in Q3 2026 sold for about **US$6.92** (Sylvera).
- **International carbon credits:** project credits from other countries and programmes, priced in their own markets.
- **CORSIA eligible emissions units:** units approved for airlines' offsetting under ICAO rules. Assessed at about **US$13.00** on 18 November 2026 (Fastmarkets).
- **Compliance allowances:** permits to emit under a cap, such as EU allowances at around **€90** in mid-November 2026.
- **Large-scale Generation Certificates (LGCs):** Australian renewable electricity certificates, each representing **one megawatt-hour**, not a tonne of CO₂. They closed at **A$7.25** on 20 November 2026 (CORE Markets). **LGCs are not carbon credits.**

**These instruments are not interchangeable** and should not be given the same price.

Within ACCUs, value can also vary with:

- **project method**, such as human-induced regeneration, savanna fire management or landfill gas
- **vintage**, the period when the abatement occurred
- **eligibility** for a buyer's purpose
- **perceived quality** and co-benefits
- **buyer requirements**

## Can Individuals and Businesses Earn Money from Australian Carbon Credits?

Yes, through **eligible projects**:

1. **Project development:** choose an activity covered by an approved ACCU Scheme method, such as regenerating native vegetation, savanna fire management or capturing landfill methane.
2. **Eligibility:** the project must meet additionality requirements and have consent from people with relevant interests in the land.
3. **Project registration** with the Clean Energy Regulator.
4. **Monitoring and reporting** according to the method.
5. **Independent audit** by a registered auditor at required points.
6. **ACCU issuance** by the CER for the verified abatement.
7. **Selling** to Safeguard facilities, the government, companies or through brokers.

Carbon storage projects must also meet **permanence obligations**, meaning the carbon must be maintained for 25 or 100 years.

**Simply planting trees or doing something environmentally friendly does not automatically create saleable ACCUs.** Projects must meet the scheme's eligibility and regulatory requirements. Income is not guaranteed. **Key differences:**

- **Pricing:** different currencies, demand drivers and rules mean prices **cannot be directly compared**.

## What Could Affect Australian Carbon Credit Values in the Future?

- **Emissions reduction policies:** Australia's targets of **43% below 2005 levels by 2030**, a **2035 target of 62–70%**, and net zero by 2050, as well as declining Safeguard baselines.
- **Corporate climate commitments:** voluntary demand depends on company targets and claims rules.
- **Future ACCU supply:** record issuance in 2026 could limit price rises.
- **Demand from regulated entities:** Safeguard facilities account for around **80% of ACCU demand**, according to the Climate Change Authority.
- **Project development:** new methods could add supply.
- **Market regulations:** reviews and rule changes could raise or lower demand.
- **International developments:** global carbon market trends and Article 6 cooperation.

Prices could rise or fall. **No price increase or investment return is guaranteed.**
